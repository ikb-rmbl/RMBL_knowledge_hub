# Centennial Capacity

*A Future Scenario in the `centennial-2027` set. Target magnitude, innovation-leaning. v2.0 under [Future Scenarios Framework v0.6](../../Future_scenarios_framework.md).*

---

## Synopsis

Centennial Capacity takes as its central contingency that the next fifteen years are when the basin's century of accumulated records becomes either newly legible or quietly stranded, and when RMBL's institutional independence either consolidates as a strategic asset or erodes under federal contraction. The campaign organizes near the upper end of the realistic bracket around four mutually reinforcing priorities: stewardship of the centennial-scale observational backbone (marmot demography, meadow phenology, snowmelt-driven plant series), substantial growth of in-house data-science and AI capacity to extract findings from a century of basin records, partnership infrastructure that engages local communities and tribal natural-resources offices as co-producers rather than recipients of basin science, and the governance and operating reserves that make independence durable rather than rhetorical. The portfolio leans innovation-heavy (roughly two-thirds), with the AI-synthesis and community-partnership investments load-bearing alongside protection of the long records they depend on. What this scenario forgoes is direct investment in new atmospheric or watershed instrumentation infrastructure, in mechanistic experimental ecology platforms, and in cross-mountain comparative partnerships; those frontiers carry forward through existing collaborations rather than new RMBL-led capacity. The scenario asks donors and the institution to commit to a posture where capacity itself — technical, community, and institutional — is the campaign's signature, and to accept that building durable connective tissue is less visible than building new things but harder to undo.

### Campaign deliverables

- **primary.** Growth of in-house data-science, geospatial, and AI capacity — the technical staff and cyberinfrastructure platforms required to make a century of accumulated basin records analytically tractable. This builds directly on the innovation-to-infrastructure flywheel that has matured RMBL's technical core over recent years and on the Knowledge Commons itself as one of that flywheel's products. Required capacities include additional FTE in data science and geospatial analysis, AI tooling for retrospective synthesis, methodological partnership capacity with guest scientists doing the analytical work, and the cyberinfrastructure that hosts and serves integrated basin data products. Sizing is the primary share of the campaign because this is where in-house RMBL capacity is genuinely load-bearing for the frontier portfolio.
- **primary.** Archival digitization and protocol-stewardship infrastructure for the centennial-scale observational backbone — the marmot demographic record approaching its centennial, the meadow phenology series, snowmelt-driven plant demography, plant-pollinator networks. Required capacities include physical archival systems, digitization of legacy field notebooks and analog records, protocol documentation and observer-succession infrastructure (largely realized through guest-scientist partnerships rather than RMBL-employed observers), and the data systems that make these records discoverable and citable. Sized as a primary share because the AI-synthesis investment is materially undermined without it.
- **secondary.** Community partnership and stakeholder co-production capacity, anchored at RMBL365 as the year-round in-town platform. Required capacities include modest dedicated FTE for partnership coordination, governance work with tribal natural-resources offices and water districts, community-engagement programming space (RMBL365 renovation at nonprofit-scale rather than new construction), and the relational infrastructure that makes co-production durable across staff turnover. Sized as a secondary share rather than primary because the work is relational and slow-building; over-resourcing it does not accelerate it.
- **secondary.** Governance and operating-reserves capacity that makes institutional independence durable rather than rhetorical. Required capacities include diversified-funding-base development capacity (the institutional muscle to win and execute external grants that feed the flywheel), operating reserves sized to weather further federal contraction without compromising research-direction independence, and the legal and governance infrastructure that protects unrestricted inquiry as a platform feature. Sized as a secondary share because the asset is protective rather than expansionary.
- **continuity_residual.** Continued maintenance of physical infrastructure at Gothic that supports the guest-scientist community — housing, lab access, field-site stewardship — at levels consistent with current operations. This is not where the campaign's distinctiveness lies, but the campaign cannot succeed if this baseline erodes.

### Frontier support strategies

- **F.cont.1.** The campaign protects the centennial backbone through archival digitization, protocol stewardship, and data infrastructure that make the long records both physically durable and analytically accessible. Observer succession is supported primarily through guest-scientist partnerships and infrastructure (housing, field access, data systems) rather than RMBL-employed observers. The frontier is treated as global infrastructure: the campaign assumes that what is uniquely valuable about these records is their length, and that no amount of new measurement substitutes for an unbroken series.
- **F.innov.3.** The campaign treats this frontier as the one where in-house RMBL capacity is most load-bearing. Investment grows the data-science and geospatial technical core (building on the existing four-person nucleus), develops AI tooling for retrospective synthesis of a century of accumulated records, and builds the cyberinfrastructure platforms — extending the Knowledge Commons itself — that host integrated data products. Methodological partnerships with guest scientists doing the analytical work are coordinated through dedicated capacity rather than ad hoc. The frontier's dependency on F.cont.1 for source material is structural: the campaign funds both together.
- **F.innov.4.** The campaign anchors community-partnership work at RMBL365 as the year-round in-town platform, with renovation at nonprofit-scale to add programming and convening space alongside existing housing and workshop functions. Modest dedicated FTE supports partnership coordination with local communities, tribal natural-resources offices, and water districts. The work aligns with RMBL's institutional-priority commitment to community service and treats co-production — rather than outreach — as the operative posture.
- **F.innov.5.** The campaign treats independence as a governance and institutional frontier with material catalytic support: diversified-funding-base capacity that reduces single-funder dependence, operating reserves that absorb shocks without compromising research-direction independence, and infrastructure investments that make RMBL attractive to guest scientists seeking unrestricted inquiry venues. The frontier is pursued in non-partisan register as a durable platform feature rather than a moment-specific defense.

### Forgone

This scenario does not fund new atmospheric or watershed integration infrastructure beyond what SAIL-era and East River partnerships already provide; the F.innov.1 atmosphere-to-bedrock frontier carries forward through external partnerships rather than new RMBL-led capacity. It does not build dedicated mountain-water-security translation capacity (F.innov.2) at a scale that would make RMBL a recognized independent translator of basin science into Mountain West water management — that work is touched only indirectly through the community-partnership frontier. It does not invest in mechanistic experimental ecology platforms (the F.cont.3 demography work, F.both.2 phenology-mismatch infrastructure, F.both.1 post-snowpack ecology) beyond protecting the observational records those programs draw on. It does not build cross-mountain comparative partnership infrastructure (F.both.3) for hosting peer-institution collaborators on basin grounds. And it does not significantly expand physical facilities at Gothic. The honest tradeoff is breadth: this scenario commits the campaign to four frontiers that share connective tissue (archival, data, community, independence) at the expense of frontiers that would require materially different investment.

### Seeds in the present

The scenario takes as foundational the in-house catalytic capacity that the innovation-to-infrastructure flywheel has already produced: the Knowledge Commons itself, RMBL's geospatial and GIS technical core (approximately four staff as of 2026), and the data systems that have grown alongside SAIL and East River partnerships. It takes RMBL365 — the in-town building acquired in 2025 — as the existing platform for year-round community-facing work rather than imagining new construction. It takes the marmot demographic record approaching its centennial (Philson and Blumstein, 2023; Blumstein, 2013), the meadow phenology series (Inouye, 2008; Inouye, 2022), and the floral-trait and pollinator work (Campbell et al., 2021; Powers et al., 2022) as the long records whose continuity and AI-assisted re-analysis the campaign organizes around. From the planning themes, it builds on the 'mechanistic field ecology' theme's emphasis on co-located long-term records, the 'integrated long-term monitoring' theme's call for harmonized cross-scale data products, and the 'co-designed basin-scale experiments' theme's recognition that community and stakeholder co-production is where many frontiers converge. Federal contraction is the operating baseline, not a hypothetical.

## Setting

This scenario takes as its central contingency the recognition that the next fifteen years will determine whether a century of accumulated basin records becomes newly analytically legible or remains partially stranded, and whether RMBL's institutional independence consolidates as a durable strategic asset or erodes under sustained federal contraction. Both questions point at the same answer: capacity — technical, community, and institutional — is what the campaign needs to build.

The decisive feature of the scenario is that capacity is mostly invisible until it does work. The campaign organizes near the upper end of the realistic bracket around four investments — archival and data infrastructure for the centennial backbone, in-house data-science and AI capacity to synthesize what the backbone records, community-partnership capacity anchored at RMBL365, and governance reserves that protect independence — that are connective tissue rather than landmark builds. The bet is that this connective tissue compounds: every AI-synthesis finding draws on the protected archive, every community-partnership engagement draws on the synthesis, every donor relationship draws on the independence the reserves protect. The frontiers carry forward not as isolated programs but as a single architecture of capacity. The cost is breadth: atmospheric, watershed-integration, and mechanistic-experimental frontiers carry forward through partnerships rather than new RMBL-led capacity. The scenario asks RMBL and its donors to accept that building things harder to undo is harder to point at than building new things, and to commit anyway.

## The arc — three phases across the primary horizon

### Phase 1: Foundations and Flywheel Acceleration (2026–2030)

The campaign's early years concentrate on the two primary investments. Archival digitization of legacy field records — marmot mark-recapture notebooks, meadow phenology sheets, decades of pollinator censuses — begins systematically, with priority given to records whose original observers are nearing the end of their active stewardship. The in-house data-science and geospatial team grows from its 2026 nucleus, with additional FTE in data engineering, AI tooling, and methodological partnership coordination. The Knowledge Commons platform extends to host integrated long-record products and to support guest-scientist analytical work at a scale the smaller technical core could not previously sustain. RMBL365 renovation begins, adding programming and convening space at nonprofit-scale alongside existing housing and workshop functions. Governance work on operating reserves and diversified-funding capacity proceeds in parallel, less visibly. By 2030, the first AI-assisted retrospective syntheses — drawing on a century of marmot data or fifty years of meadow phenology — begin appearing in guest-scientist publications, with RMBL technical staff as methodological co-authors. The flywheel that produced the Knowledge Commons accelerates: external grants for sensing and cyberinfrastructure development now compete more strongly because the in-house capacity to execute them has grown.

### Phase 2: Synthesis and Co-Production (2031–2035)

The middle years are when the campaign's investments begin compounding. AI-assisted synthesis of the long records yields findings prior generations could not extract — patterns in marmot social-structure-by-climate interactions across the full demographic record, phenological-mismatch signatures resolved at the individual-plant level across decades, pollinator-network rewiring at temporal resolutions the field-data-alone era could not support. Guest scientists working with RMBL's technical core publish at higher rates and with stronger methodological infrastructure than the smaller-staff era allowed. Community partnerships, anchored at RMBL365, mature: water districts, tribal natural-resources offices, and county-level planning bodies engage basin science not as recipients of findings but as co-producers of questions, drawing on the planning themes' recognition that stakeholder pull for evidence is already strong. The governance and reserves work, less visible than the analytical output, has done its job: when federal funding fluctuates further mid-decade, RMBL does not contract its research-direction independence to absorb the shock. Cross-frontier connections — between the long-records synthesis and community-partnership work, between independence reserves and guest-scientist recruitment — become legible as a single institutional posture.

### Phase 3: Centennial Consolidation (2036–2040)

The final phase coincides with the marmot study's centennial and with the meadow phenology record passing its sixtieth year. The campaign's investments mature into a state where the basin's long records are protected by institutional architecture rather than by individual observer commitment, where AI-assisted synthesis is a routine capability rather than an experimental one, and where community co-production is a recognized feature of how basin science gets done rather than a programmatic add-on. RMBL365 functions year-round as a recognized in-town anchor for both community engagement and guest-scientist staging. The diversified funding base built over the prior decade carries a larger share of operations than federal sources, with the campaign endowment supporting the connective-tissue capacity that external grants do not naturally fund. By 2040, the institutional question the campaign was organized around — whether the next horizon would inherit a durable basin-science architecture or a contingent one — has been answered durably. The cost remains visible: atmospheric integration, watershed instrumentation, and cross-mountain partnership work have carried forward through external partnerships rather than RMBL-led capacity, and those frontiers are less recognizably RMBL's signature than they would have been under a different portfolio.

## Lines of inquiry

The research themes that mature through this period are those whose advancement depends most on the connective tissue the campaign builds. The centennial marmot record, read through molecular age clocks and AI-assisted social-network analysis, becomes the canonical mammalian dataset for testing how social structure mediates climate vulnerability across a full century. The meadow phenology record, joined with snowmelt and weather reconstructions through AI-assisted retrospective work, resolves mechanistic questions about plasticity-versus-evolution at temporal resolutions the field-only era could not support, extending the Anderson et al. (2012) line of inquiry into its mature phase. Plant-pollinator network science draws on a century of accumulated observations to ask network-rewiring questions at decadal resolution. Community-co-produced science around water, snow, and pollinators matures as basin findings route into county-level planning and tribal natural-resources decisions through durable partnership infrastructure rather than ad hoc consultation. What does not mature through this scenario at the same rate is mechanistic experimental ecology requiring new infrastructure (mechanistic phenology platforms, post-snowpack experimental ecology), atmosphere-to-bedrock integration beyond what SAIL-era partnerships sustain, and cross-mountain comparative work requiring new partnership infrastructure. Those lines continue but through external leadership rather than RMBL-led capacity.

## Moments of choice within the primary horizon

1. **2028 — Whether the campaign's structure allocates the larger share of resources to durable in-house capacity (technical FTE, data systems, governance reserves) versus to capital builds with named-gift visibility.** *Shared inflection: `endowment-capital-split-2028`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and Development & Advancement team
   - Alternatives: A capital-heavier structure would produce more visible named-gift opportunities and likely close faster, but would underweight the technical and governance capacity the frontier portfolio requires. A capacity-heavier structure asks donors to fund less visible work and requires more sophisticated donor cultivation.
   - Scenario assumption: The campaign weights toward durable capacity, accepting the harder donor case in exchange for the frontier portfolio's coherence.

2. **2029 — Whether AI-tooling development for retrospective synthesis is built primarily as in-house capacity that guest scientists draw on, or as external partnerships where guest scientists bring their own AI capacity to basin data.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL technical leadership and in-house data-science staff
   - Alternatives: An external-partnership posture would lower in-house staffing costs and let RMBL stay closer to its catalytic-infrastructure tradition, but would leave the most distinctive analytical capabilities outside the institution. An in-house posture builds load-bearing capacity but requires sustained technical hiring.
   - Scenario assumption: The campaign builds the AI synthesis capacity in-house, recognizing it as a frontier where the institution's catalytic role requires institutional ownership of the tooling.

3. **2031 — Whether community-partnership work formalizes through standing co-production agreements with shared governance, or stays in a lighter-touch consultation register.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and community partners (water districts, tribal natural-resources offices, county planning bodies)
   - Alternatives: Lighter-touch consultation preserves RMBL's responsive-to-guest-scientists posture without taking on partnership obligations that constrain research direction. Standing co-production agreements deepen community relevance but require sustained relational capacity and accept some constraint on research framing.
   - Scenario assumption: Standing co-production agreements form with several key partners, anchored at RMBL365, accepting the relational obligations as a feature of the institutional-priority commitment to community.

4. **2032 — Whether the campaign's mid-horizon stewardship priorities double down on the centennial records or rebalance toward newer research frontiers as federal funding evolves.** *Shared inflection: `stewardship-priority-2032`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and board
   - Alternatives: Doubling down on the centennial records preserves the campaign's distinctive global infrastructure claim but constrains responsiveness to emerging science. Rebalancing keeps the portfolio responsive but risks the centennial commitment becoming a stewardship-of-last-resort posture rather than a strategic one.
   - Scenario assumption: Stewardship of the centennial backbone is reaffirmed as primary, with the AI-synthesis investment justified as making that backbone newly productive rather than as a pivot away from it.

5. **2034 — Whether operating reserves built through the campaign are deployed to absorb a federal-funding shock or held to protect longer-horizon independence.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and Development team
   - Alternatives: Deployment preserves near-term research activity through a contraction but reduces the reserves' capacity to protect research-direction independence across multiple shocks. Holding maintains the institutional-independence asset but accepts some short-term contraction of activity.
   - Scenario assumption: Reserves are partially deployed but the core protective function is preserved, with the campaign's diversified-funding-base capacity carrying most of the shock-absorption work.

6. **2037 — Whether in-house AI-synthesis capacity, having matured, is offered as a service to peer mountain field stations or kept as RMBL-internal capability.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL technical leadership and guest-scientist community
   - Alternatives: Offering it as a service extends RMBL's catalytic role across the mountain-station network and could attract new funding, but stretches in-house staff. Keeping it internal preserves the capacity for basin work without dilution.
   - Scenario assumption: The capacity is offered selectively to peer institutions through formal partnerships rather than broadly, treating it as a basin-anchored asset with cross-mountain reach.

7. **2039 — Whether the next campaign or strategic-planning horizon inherits the capacity-first posture or pivots to a build-first posture with the connective tissue now in place.** *Shared inflection: `next-horizon-2040`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and Development team
   - Alternatives: Continuing the capacity-first posture deepens what this campaign began but risks visible-build fatigue among donors. Pivoting to a build-first posture takes advantage of the capacity now in place but risks underweighting the maintenance the capacity requires.
   - Scenario assumption: The next horizon inherits the capacity-first posture as institutional baseline, with new builds layered on top rather than substituting for capacity maintenance.

## Audience lens — research

Scientists working on the basin's long records find their work supported by a level of in-house technical infrastructure that the smaller-staff era could not sustain. Guest scientists collaborating on marmot, meadow-phenology, or pollinator-network analysis have access to AI-tooling and integrated data products through methodological partnerships with RMBL's technical core rather than building those tools alone. Researchers whose questions require a century of accumulated context find that context analytically tractable rather than archivally scattered. Community-engaged researchers find standing co-production relationships rather than ad hoc consultation arrangements. Researchers seeking unrestricted inquiry venues find RMBL's independence protected by institutional architecture, not just by tradition. What scientists working on frontiers outside this portfolio find is that those frontiers continue through other partnerships and other institutions; the basin remains accessible to them, but the campaign's distinctive support runs elsewhere.

## Audience lens — institution

RMBL becomes, through this frontier portfolio, an institution whose distinctive capacity is connective tissue: archival, analytical, relational, and governance infrastructure that holds disparate guest-scientist work together as a coherent basin science. The in-house technical core, having grown from its 2026 nucleus, is recognized as load-bearing rather than supporting — the AI-assisted synthesis capability is genuinely RMBL's, not borrowed. RMBL365 functions as the year-round in-town anchor for community-facing work, complementing rather than competing with Gothic. The institution's independence is no longer only a tradition but a protected platform feature, visible to guest scientists choosing where to base unrestricted inquiry. The cost: RMBL is not, through this scenario, the recognized basin platform for atmospheric integration, mechanistic experimental ecology, or cross-mountain comparative work; those identities belong to partners. What the institution becomes is more durable than expansive, and its distinctive shape is more legible at the level of how things compound than at the level of any single landmark build.

## Audience lens — donor

Your contribution joins an effort to build the kind of institutional capacity that compounds — the archival, technical, community, and governance infrastructure that makes a century of accumulated basin science newly productive and durably independent. It is part of what enables a centennial mammalian demographic record to yield findings prior generations could not extract; what enables co-production with water districts, tribal natural-resources offices, and the community RMBL has served for nearly a century to become routine rather than occasional; and what enables institutional independence to remain a platform feature rather than a contingent one. The work is connective tissue more than landmark builds — capacity that does its work mostly invisibly, that is harder to point at than a new facility, and that is also harder to undo. You are part of a posture that treats the basin's long records as the rare global asset they are and treats RMBL's distinctive operating model — small catalytic staff, broad responsive infrastructure, community as institutional priority — as worth deepening rather than transforming.

## Overlay robustness

**Central case.** Under gradual federal contraction, gradual AI evolution, and RCP6.0 climate trajectories, this scenario's investments compound roughly as designed. The diversified-funding base built through the campaign carries an increasing share of operations through the 2030s without forcing research-direction compromises. AI-tooling for retrospective synthesis matures along a trajectory that in-house capacity can absorb without overcommitting to specific technical bets. Climate-driven changes in the basin's long records — earlier snowmelt, shifting phenology, marmot social structure responses — become the substantive content of the synthesis work rather than threats to it. Community partnerships mature at the relational pace such work requires. The connective-tissue investments are coherent with the central-case environment.

**Stress case — Sharper federal contraction with reduced philanthropic capacity.** Under sharper federal contraction combined with reduced philanthropic capacity (a recessionary stress), the campaign's mid-horizon trajectory tightens. Operating reserves built early in the period absorb more of the shock than designed, and some discretionary capacity (mid-tier technical hiring, RMBL365 programming expansion) slows. The centennial-backbone protection and core in-house AI capacity hold, because they were sized as primary priorities and because their costs are partly capital rather than recurring. Community-partnership work continues but at lighter touch than the standing co-production posture envisioned. Independence reserves perform their protective function, but the institution emerges from the period with less expansive capacity than the central case projects.

**Stress case — Rapid AI capability evolution outpaces in-house adoption.** If AI capability evolves faster than the in-house data-science team can absorb, the campaign's investment in load-bearing in-house AI capacity is partly stranded against frontier tools maintained externally. The scenario adapts by shifting toward methodological-partnership posture for AI work while keeping in-house capacity focused on data infrastructure, archival access, and the basin-specific tooling that is genuinely durable. The synthesis findings still emerge but with more guest-scientist analytical leadership than the central case envisions. The frontier still advances; the RMBL-specific catalytic role is somewhat more modest.

**Stress case — Accelerated climate change crosses basin thresholds early.** Under accelerated climate change — basin snowpack thresholds crossed in the early 2030s rather than late — the long records become more rather than less valuable, but the scenario's lack of investment in post-snowpack experimental ecology (F.both.1) leaves the basin community more dependent on external partnerships for studying transformed systems. The centennial backbone documents the transition with high fidelity; the campaign's AI-synthesis capacity makes the transition newly legible. Community partnerships become more urgent as basin water and ecological changes accelerate, and the RMBL365 anchor proves more valuable than central-case framing assumed. The scenario's forgone investment in atmosphere-to-bedrock integration is felt: RMBL is a partner in studying the transformed basin, not the platform for it.

## Plausibility caveats

This scenario assumes that federal funding contracts gradually rather than collapses, that philanthropic capacity in RMBL's donor base supports a campaign near the upper end of the realistic bracket, and that in-house technical staff can be recruited and retained in a competitive market for data-science and AI talent at the salaries a small nonprofit can sustain. It assumes that AI-tooling for retrospective synthesis evolves at a rate the in-house team can absorb, and that the long records' analytical value is high enough to justify the digitization and platform investment ahead of the findings that prove it. It assumes that community-partnership work matures at the relational pace such work requires and is not derailed by political or institutional turbulence outside RMBL's control.

The structural blind spot is that capacity-first investment is harder to evaluate at horizon-end than build-first investment: a campaign that builds connective tissue and then declares success is asking donors and the institution to trust that compounding effects are real rather than rhetorical. A second blind spot is that the scenario's forgone investment in atmospheric, watershed, and mechanistic-experimental frontiers may, in retrospect, prove to have been where basin science's center of gravity actually moved — leaving RMBL well-positioned for the frontiers it chose but underweight in the frontiers the field most rewarded. A third is that institutional-independence framing, however non-partisan in register, is read partisanly by some audiences in a polarized environment, and the scenario's governance investments may face political contestation the planning process cannot fully anticipate.

## Looking further out: 2040–2050

*Speculative. Lower resolution than the primary horizon.*

Looking out toward 2050, speculatively: the marmot record passes 115 years and the meadow phenology record passes 70, with AI-assisted analytical frameworks that are now routine reading these records at resolutions the 2020s could not access. The in-house technical core has stabilized at a scale that supports both basin work and selective partnerships with peer mountain field stations; the basin is part of a small cross-mountain data-and-synthesis network anchored on its own terms rather than as a node in a federal program. RMBL365 has matured into a recognized year-round community anchor whose programming reflects two decades of co-production with water districts, tribal natural-resources offices, and county planning bodies; basin science routes into community decisions through durable channels rather than ad hoc consultation. The institution's independence is, by 2050, less a campaign theme than an inherited fact: the governance and reserves work of the late 2020s and early 2030s has done what such work does when it works, which is to become invisible. The forgone work — atmospheric integration, mechanistic experimental platforms, cross-mountain comparative infrastructure — has continued through other institutions, and the basin's contribution to those frontiers runs through partnership rather than RMBL leadership. The next horizon inherits an institution that bet on capacity over breadth and an architecture of basin science that compounds rather than expands. Whether that bet reads as wisdom or as missed opportunity depends on questions the 2040s will answer that the 2026 planning process cannot.
