### Campaign deliverables

- **Endowed Cyberinfrastructure and Catalytic Capacity Fund (~$2M endowment, ~$100K/yr sustainable draw).** Permanently funds the in-house data-science and cyberinfrastructure capacity that the innovation-to-infrastructure flywheel has built over recent years — the staff and operational support behind data platforms, AI-assisted synthesis tools, and the Knowledge Commons. Endows what has been grant-funded so that the basin's catalytic data capability survives independently of federal funding cycles. Funds capacity expansion in adjacent areas (sensing, instrumentation development) where guest-scientist demand is highest.
- **Endowed Community Engagement Fund (~$1.5M endowment, ~$75K/yr).** Funds approximately 1.5 FTE of dedicated community-engagement and translation staff at scales realistic to a small-staff nonprofit. Their work: sustained relationships with Gunnison County, neighboring counties, tribal natural-resources offices, water districts, and Forest Service planning staff; communicating basin findings in registers those partners can use; co-producing research priorities with community partners; serving the community-priority commitment that is one of RMBL's institutional defining features.
- **Independence Operating Reserves (~$0.5M endowment, ~$25K/yr).** Unrestricted operating-support endowment that contributes to RMBL's institutional capacity to remain independent of restrictions on inquiry that some peer institutions face. A small dollar amount but a meaningful institutional commitment.
- **Critical-Path Stewardship Fund (~$1.5M endowment, ~$75K/yr).** Protects only the most fragile centennial records at minimum-viable level — the marmot record and the meadow-warming experiment most directly. Other long-running programs continue to depend on competitive grants; some smaller continuous datasets accept the risk of grant-cycle vulnerability through the period.
- **Capital — RMBL365 renovation (~$1M).** RMBL365 — the year-round in-town venue in Crested Butte that RMBL acquired in 2025, currently providing housing, workshop, and community-engagement space — is renovated to add laboratory space, staging space for guest scientists, dedicated rooms for community-engagement programming, and offices for the cyberinfrastructure and community-engagement staff. Capitalizes on an existing institutional asset rather than building new; anchors RMBL's expanded community presence in a venue already designed for year-round operation.
- **Selective Gothic refurbishment (~$0.5M capital).** Targeted housing and lab improvements at the existing Gothic core, prioritized around what the expanded program in residency requires.
- **Sensing and instrumentation development support (~$0.5M capital).** Extends the in-house instrumentation flywheel — modest investment in tooling and prototype-stage capabilities that the catalytic technical staff can develop into externally-funded next-stage instrumentation work.

### Frontier support strategies

- **F.innov.3 AI-assisted retrospective synthesis of long basin records** — primary, load-bearing. The endowed cyberinfrastructure capacity is the mechanism by which this frontier advances. In-house data-science staff develop tools, partner with guest-scientist users, and produce the synthesis work that prior generations could not have undertaken. The Knowledge Commons is the architectural ancestor of what this frontier matures.
- **F.innov.4 Community-science partnerships and stakeholder co-production** — primary. The endowed Community Engagement Fund is the mechanism. The renovated RMBL365 is its physical anchor, with the campaign capital adding the dedicated community-engagement programming rooms, lab and staging spaces, and staff offices the function requires. This frontier aligns with RMBL's community-priority commitment directly, and the RMBL365 acquisition is one recent institutional expression of that commitment.
- **F.innov.5 Institutional independence as platform for inquiry** — explicit. The Independence Operating Reserves, the diversification of the funding base implicit in any successful campaign, and the public visibility of RMBL's independence commitment all contribute. This is a frontier the current moment has elevated, and the campaign engages it directly.
- **F.cont.1 Centennial-scale ecological observation as global infrastructure** — secondary. The Critical-Path Stewardship Fund protects what's most fragile. Other long-running programs remain grant-dependent — a real tradeoff that public scenarios are required to articulate.

### Forgone

The campaign does not fund: comprehensive centennial-record continuity insulation (only critical-path is protected); the Continuity Fellowships at career-track scale (succession depends on a combination of partial endowment support, postdoctoral funding, and individual researcher commitment); comprehensive archival digitization (selective only); broad capital refurbishment of Gothic facilities. Some long-running programs not in the critical-path scope may lapse or shift to less-frequent measurement schedules over the period under grant pressure. The basin's long-record portfolio in 2040 under Capacity is somewhat more focused than it would be under Stewardship. This is the tradeoff the campaign asks donors and staff to accept in exchange for the transformation the innovation investments enable.

### Seeds in the present

The in-house catalytic capacity produced by recent years of innovation-to-infrastructure flywheel work (geospatial data scientist, GIS manager, instrumentation technicians; cyberinfrastructure platforms; the Knowledge Commons); the East River testbed and SAIL atmospheric campaign (Hubbard et al., 2018; Carroll et al., 2018); the marmot demographic record (Blumstein, 2013); the meadow-warming experimental lineage (Harte et al., 1995); the phenology-mismatch tradition (Anderson et al., 2012; Inouye, 2008); existing community-engagement relationships with Gunnison County, neighboring counties, water districts, and tribal partners that have developed in recent years and that the Community Engagement Fund extends.

## Setting

This scenario takes as its central contingency three coordinated near-term choices. RMBL positions the Centennial Campaign around capacity — both the in-house catalytic capacity that recent years have built, and the community-engagement capacity that the current moment makes especially salient. The campaign closes at approximately $7.5M, near the ceiling of the realistic bracket. The resulting endowment and capital commitments are weighted toward transformation: making permanent what the innovation-to-infrastructure flywheel has shown is possible, and extending RMBL's public-facing capacities into roles the institution has not previously inhabited at scale.

None of these is automatic. The continuity-leaning pitch (Stewardship) is the quieter and lower-execution-risk choice. The transformation pitch (Capacity) is louder, more demanding to communicate, and requires donors who value building over preserving. The campaign could close at a lower magnitude that forces consolidation onto one emphasis. The scenario plays out under these choices going as articulated.

The decisive feature is what an endowed catalytic-capacity commitment means at this institutional scale. Over recent years RMBL has cultivated a productive externally-funded flywheel in which grants build in-house capacity (sensing, cyberinfrastructure, data systems), which catalyzes the broader guest-scientist community's work, which supports the next round of grants. This Knowledge Commons is one product of that flywheel. The Centennial Capacity scenario endows the capacity rather than relying on continued grant-cycle wins to maintain it, and extends the capacity into adjacent domains — community engagement, sensing development, AI-assisted synthesis — that the current moment makes especially valuable.

The campaign exists in a fundraising environment shaped by federal contraction. The past year's disruptions have made diversified funding bases more strategically necessary than they were a decade earlier and have made RMBL's institutional independence more visible as a strategic asset relative to peer institutions facing direct restrictions on their inquiry. The Capacity scenario engages all three of these — funding-base diversification, in-house capacity that does not depend on federal grants, and explicit institutional-independence investment — as part of an integrated transformation.

This is not a quiet scenario. It is the one with the most upside, the most execution complexity, and the most demand on the institution's ability to absorb growth without losing what makes it distinctive.

## The arc — three phases across the primary horizon

### Phase 1: Campaign and flywheel activation (2026–2030)

The Centennial Campaign launches in 2026 with the capacity emphasis articulated — continuity at critical-path level and transformation through catalytic-capacity endowment and community-engagement infrastructure. The campaign closes in 2029 with approximately $7.5M committed.

The endowed Cyberinfrastructure and Catalytic Capacity Fund is established in 2029 with first sustainable draw of approximately $100K reaching the in-house data-science and cyberinfrastructure capacity in 2030. The work this funds — the data platforms, AI-assisted tools, the Knowledge Commons-descended infrastructure — transitions from grant-funded to durably-funded over the course of 2030–2031. This is the most direct extension of the innovation-to-infrastructure flywheel: capacity that was previously rebuilt every grant cycle now persists between them.

The endowed Community Engagement Fund supports the first dedicated community-engagement and translation staff person from 2030 onward; the second comes online in 2031. Early work focuses on relationship-building with Gunnison County, neighboring counties, and the regional partners that RMBL has worked with on a project basis but now engages systematically — not yet major outputs, but operational infrastructure for the work to come.

Capital projects begin almost immediately on RMBL365 renovation design and selective Gothic refurbishment. The RMBL365 renovation timeline runs through 2032 — designing and building out the additional functions (laboratory, staging, community programming rooms, staff offices) inside the year-round in-town space the institution acquired in 2025. Sensing and instrumentation development support flows to the technical staff to extend the basin's instrumentation flywheel into adjacent measurement capabilities.

A meaningful early signal arrives in 2030: visiting research teams begin scheduling residencies in anticipation of the renovated RMBL365's expanded functions coming online, attracted by the combination of relatively-good data infrastructure (relative to grant-pressured peer institutions), the institutional independence the basin offers, and a year-round in-town venue that makes residencies workable outside the short Gothic summer. The basin's reputation as a hosting site for collaborative work that depends on durable infrastructure begins to consolidate.

### Phase 2: Capacity build-out and partnership development (2031–2035)

The campaign's commitments mature during this window. The renovated RMBL365 comes online in 2032 — a meaningful institutional milestone that becomes the visible face of the campaign's transformation. The Crested Butte presence transitions from the year-round hostel-style configuration the institution acquired in 2025 into a year-round, multi-function venue serving laboratory, staging, community-engagement, and staff-office roles. Selective Gothic refurbishment completes in 2031. The endowed cyberinfrastructure capacity is fully operational from 2031 onward.

The Community Engagement Fund's staff produce their first major outputs in 2033–2034: a water-management briefing distributed through Colorado River Basin planning channels, an agricultural-adaptation guidance document distributed through Gunnison County extension, an input to a Forest Service planning revision, sustained partnership documentation with one or two tribal natural-resources offices. None of these requires the institution to be larger than it is; they require the institution to be more publicly present than it has been, and the endowed staffing is what makes that present.

The endowed cyberinfrastructure capacity produces what becomes its most visible mid-period contribution: a substantial expansion of AI-assisted retrospective synthesis tooling that guest scientists across the basin's research traditions begin to use routinely. By 2034 the data infrastructure RMBL operates is recognized as among the best at any comparable field station. This recognition itself attracts collaborations and grants — the flywheel continues to spin, now from a more durable base.

The Critical-Path Stewardship Fund operates from 2030 onward, supporting the marmot record and the meadow-warming experiment specifically. Succession planning for the founding observers happens through a combination of fund support, postdoctoral funding, and individual researcher commitment — at sustained-program continuity but with more residual reliance on grant funding than Stewardship would provide. Some smaller continuous datasets accept the risk of grant-cycle vulnerability, with the understanding that the campaign's scope did not extend to comprehensive insulation.

In this same window, the first execution-stress signals appear. Managing the dual emphasis — significant in-house capacity expansion alongside the new community-engagement function alongside selected continuity protection — requires more institutional bandwidth than leadership initially anticipated. Donor cultivation now spans multiple program-specific stories. The board's attention is more distributed. Staff at RMBL navigate the existing place-based research culture alongside the more publicly-facing community-engagement culture the Engagement Fund creates. By 2035, the institutional question of whether the multi-emphasis approach is sustainable at its current ambition becomes visible.

### Phase 3: Compounding capacity and impact (2036–2040)

The campaign's transformative effects become evident during this window. The endowed in-house catalytic capacity is recognized externally as a distinctive RMBL contribution. The data platforms, AI synthesis tools, and cyberinfrastructure RMBL operates — direct descendants of the innovation-to-infrastructure flywheel work that produced this Knowledge Commons — are now part of how mountain-system science is done by the broader research community.

The Community Engagement Fund's staff have produced demonstrable impact by 2040. Identifiable Forest Service planning provisions, water-management decisions, agricultural-adaptation guidance, and tribal-partner collaborations are traceable to basin science inputs the Fund coordinated. By 2040, the community-engagement function is sufficiently established that its absence would be visibly felt by partners who have come to depend on it. RMBL has become one of the institutions that the regional community looks to for science-informed input, in ways it had been only inconsistently before.

The Independence Operating Reserves contribute through the period in ways that are less visible day-to-day but durable across institutional shocks. The reserves cushion against funding cycles and contribute to RMBL's institutional resilience — a small dollar amount but a meaningful commitment.

The Critical-Path Stewardship Fund continues to protect the most fragile continuity programs. The marmot record continues toward its centennial. Other long-running programs remain intact through a combination of fund support, grant work, and individual researcher commitment, with somewhat more variance than under Stewardship. By the late 2030s, a few legacy datasets that were not in the critical-path scope have lapsed or transitioned to less-frequent measurement schedules. The basin's long-record portfolio in 2040 is more focused and somewhat less comprehensive than it would be under Stewardship. Whether this represents disciplined prioritization or accumulating loss depends on the perspective.

By 2040, the campaign's institutional inflection point arrives: whether to continue inside the multi-emphasis structure (accepting ongoing execution stress as the new institutional shape), launch a second campaign to deepen the continuity protection that Capacity left under-funded, or consolidate around the in-house capacity emphasis the period has demonstrated viable.

## Lines of inquiry that mature

The atmosphere-to-bedrock work begun with the East River testbed (Hubbard et al., 2018) and the SAIL campaign continues through grant-funded research using the data infrastructure RMBL's endowed capacity operates. The campaign's contribution to this frontier is the durable data systems and the in-house technical capacity on which the research depends — not directly the research itself, which is guest-scientist-driven, but the catalytic infrastructure without which the research could not be sustained at its current depth.

The AI-assisted retrospective synthesis of long basin records matures from possibility to routine. By 2035 the question "can we extract findings from a century of records that prior generations could not analyze?" is answered structurally: yes, and the answers compound through the period. This frontier becomes a distinctive RMBL contribution.

The translation between basin findings and Mountain West management decisions becomes institutionalized through the Community Engagement Fund. By 2040, basin science routinely informs water-allocation conversations, Forest Service revisions, and agricultural extension guidance. The function does not generate new findings; it changes the rate at which existing and emerging findings reach decisions. The compounding effect is that basin research is more visibly consequential to the surrounding region than it was in 2025.

Some lines of inquiry visible in the late 2020s do not develop as fully under Capacity as they would under Stewardship. Some smaller continuous datasets accept grant-cycle vulnerability. Continuity succession is somewhat more strained. The basin's long-record portfolio is more focused and somewhat less comprehensive at 2040 than the alternative scenario produces. These are real tradeoffs the campaign asks donors and staff to accept.

## Moments of choice within the primary horizon

Seven inflection points, all within the 15-year window.

1. **2028 endowment-vs-capital split.** *Shared inflection: `endowment-capital-split-2028`.* Made by RMBL leadership and board during the campaign itself. Whether to commit closer to $5M endowment / $2.5M capital (as assumed) or different splits. The 65/35 endowment/capital ratio assumed here matches Stewardship's but has different distribution across funds. Reasonable alternatives produce different versions of the scenario.

2. **2029 capacity-vs-stewardship allocation choice.** *Distinctive to Capacity-style scenarios.* Made by board and leadership as the campaign closes. How the campaign's commitments distribute across in-house catalytic capacity, community engagement, independence reserves, and critical-path stewardship. The 27/20/7/20 allocation (out of $7.5M total) assumed in this scenario gives capacity meaningful primary status while keeping stewardship at critical-path levels. Reasonable arguments for different distributions; the choice has compounding effects through the period.

3. **2031 community-partnership-design choice.** *Distinctive to Capacity.* Made by Community Engagement Fund leadership and RMBL administration as the function reaches operational capacity. Whether the function focuses its limited capacity (1.5 FTE) primarily on regional water-management partnerships, on Gunnison County agricultural and community partnerships, on tribal natural-resources collaboration, or on a distributed mix. The realistic answer is a distributed mix, but the primary emphasis materially affects which relationships deepen.

4. **2032 stewardship-priority sequence.** *Shared inflection: `stewardship-priority-2032`.* Made by RMBL leadership in consultation with the senior observers and in-house technical staff. Which programs the Critical-Path Stewardship Fund prioritizes. With $75K/yr to allocate, the choice is more constrained than under Stewardship; the sequence forces explicit decisions about which records receive sufficient support to weather observer succession and which are accepted as more grant-dependent.

5. **2034 capacity-scope decision.** *Distinctive to Capacity.* Made as the in-house catalytic capacity matures into its endowed steady-state. Whether to use the cyberinfrastructure capacity to extend into adjacent domains (more sensing, more instrumentation, broader data infrastructure) or to deepen the existing capabilities (better tools, more integration, more sustained partner support). Expansion increases the basin's catalytic surface area; deepening makes the existing capabilities more powerful.

6. **2037 execution-stress resolution.** *Distinctive to Capacity.* Made by board and leadership as the cumulative institutional cost of running the multi-emphasis approach becomes more apparent. Whether to consolidate (allow one emphasis to emerge as institutionally dominant, sustaining the others at reduced visibility), continue with intentional multi-emphasis (accepting ongoing strain as institutional shape), or seek supplementary fundraising to address execution capacity directly. The scenario assumes intentional multi-emphasis continues; both other paths are defensible.

7. **2040 next-horizon framing.** *Shared inflection: `next-horizon-2040`.* Made at the primary-horizon boundary. How the campaign's transformation should shape the next visioning cycle.

## Audience lens — research

A scientist starting at the basin in 2027 has more pathways than under Stewardship. They could anchor in continuity work (with somewhat less institutional support than Stewardship would provide). They could affiliate with the in-house catalytic capacity, doing data-systems work that supports the broader research community. They could engage the community-engagement function, learning the discipline of moving research findings into management decisions. They could affiliate with sensing and instrumentation development. By 2040 they are mid-career and have chosen — explicitly or by accumulated emphasis — which institutional shape their work primarily inhabits. The choices are richer than Stewardship's; the path is less clearly defined.

A senior guest scientist who has been bringing collaborative work to the basin sees in 2030 that the data infrastructure their projects depend on is now endowed rather than grant-fragile, and that the basin has visible community-engagement function that they can partner with for translation. They are more willing to commit multi-year research programs partly because the infrastructure is durable and partly because the basin offers visible institutional independence in a moment where peer institutions face restrictions on their work.

## Audience lens — institution

RMBL under Centennial Capacity is, by 2040, a materially different institution from RMBL in 2025. It operates expanded in-house data and cyberinfrastructure capacity routinely. It hosts a dedicated community-engagement function that produces outputs reaching Mountain West decisions. It operates the renovated RMBL365 as a year-round, multi-function venue and as physical evidence of its more publicly-present role. It maintains a more focused (though sub-comprehensive) portfolio of long-running programs. It manages a more complex donor-relationship structure across more program-specific commitments. Its board navigates more decisions per cycle than during the continuity-only era.

The institution's distinctive shape — small-staff nonprofit, guest-scientist-driven research, catalytic-infrastructure commitments, community-priority — is preserved in core but expanded around. The community-priority commitment, which was real but quiet in 2025, becomes more visibly institutional through the Community Engagement Fund's work. The institutional independence, which the campaign explicitly endows reserves to protect, becomes a more publicly-visible feature of what makes RMBL distinctive.

Whether this transformation is the institution's strongest moment or the prelude to overextension depends on choices made in the 2037 execution-stress moment and on external contingencies the campaign itself does not control. Capacity creates the conditions for transformation; whether the institution flourishes inside those conditions is the open question the scenario leaves unresolved.

## Audience lens — donor

A donor contributing to Centennial Capacity joins an institutional transformation in progress. Their gift can be tied to specific commitments with concrete near-term outputs: an endowed position in the Cyberinfrastructure capacity, a portion of the RMBL365 renovation (e.g., a named lab or community-engagement space), a portion of the Community Engagement Fund, infrastructure for sensing development. The visible impact of their gift is faster than under Stewardship — the renovated RMBL365 comes online in 2032, community-engagement outputs reach decisions by 2034, in-house capacity is endowed by 2031.

The tradeoff donors should understand: more execution risk, more dependence on RMBL's ability to manage multi-emphasis well, less of the durable-quietude that Stewardship offers. A donor to Capacity is part of building something at greater scale than RMBL has previously inhabited, whose successful operation depends on institutional capacities the small-staff nonprofit is developing in real time. Donors whose values lead them to bet on transformation will find Capacity their natural campaign. Donors whose values lead them to preserve what has been built will find Stewardship more comfortable.

This is a public scenario read by general readers as well as donors. To a reader who is not contemplating a gift, Capacity describes one form a successful Centennial Campaign could take — a more transformative form that extends the in-house catalytic capacity flywheel into durable institutional infrastructure and that makes RMBL more publicly present in its region.

## Overlay robustness

**Central case (gradual federal contraction continuing × gradual AI evolution × RCP6.0 climate trajectory).** The scenario as described. The endowed in-house capacity extends the flywheel; the Community Engagement Fund produces sustained translation work; the institution by 2040 is recognizably transformed while preserving critical-path continuity.

**Stress case: sharp federal contraction.** Federal grant pipeline collapses below currently-contracted levels. The endowed cyberinfrastructure capacity becomes more strategically valuable — it is precisely the kind of work that would have been grant-fragile and is now durable. The flywheel's external-grant feed contracts but the in-house capacity it built persists through endowment. Some of the work that previously depended on federal grants relocates to grant-independent execution through the endowed capacity. Capacity is more resilient to sharp federal contraction than its risk profile might suggest, because the campaign anticipated exactly this scenario in its commitments.

**Stress case: severe climate trajectory (RCP8.5-like).** Basin systems change faster than the scenario assumes. The in-house data and AI synthesis capacity becomes particularly valuable for documenting transformation and connecting basin records to global Earth-system models. The Community Engagement Fund's work shifts toward adaptation guidance and translation of accelerating findings to local decisions. Capacity adapts well to severe climate trajectories because its central commitments are about producing and translating timely findings, not about preserving baseline measurements.

**Stress case: AI economy disruption.** If AI tools fundamentally change the economics of in-house data-science capacity (or make synthesis-by-AI displace in-situ measurement), the campaign's heavy investment in in-house catalytic capacity becomes either highly valuable (if RMBL's combination of in-situ records, in-house data-science, and community partnerships becomes a distinctive integrated offering that AI alone cannot replicate) or strategically misaligned (if AI synthesis becomes cheap enough that the basin's catalytic capacity loses its differentiation). The scenario assumes the former.

## Plausibility caveats

The scenario is **strongly contingent** on the campaign closing at $7.5M with the multi-emphasis articulated clearly enough to attract donors valuing both continuity and transformation. The campaign could fall short of $7.5M, in which case the multi-emphasis becomes forced consolidation onto whichever direction the realized donor base supports most strongly. The campaign could succeed at $7.5M but with imbalanced donor preferences that skew the institutional outcome away from the balanced case described.

The execution-stress assumption is real and consequential. The scenario assumes RMBL's leadership and board can absorb the management complexity of running multi-emphasis at $7.5M scale without losing institutional coherence. This is not a small assumption. Field stations RMBL's size have, historically, struggled with institutional expansion at this pace. Whether RMBL specifically can absorb it depends on leadership capacity, board composition, and staff retention through the period.

The "in-house catalytic capacity is load-bearing" assumption depends on whether AI synthesis tools (which evolve through the period) reinforce or displace the basin's distinctive in-house capability. The scenario assumes reinforcement; an alternative future is possible.

The Community Engagement Fund's policy impact depends on partnerships, political cycles, and the quality of basin science's translation-readiness — none of which the campaign itself controls. The function could reach decisions at the scale described or could find its outputs less consequential than anticipated. Sustained relationships with the regional partners require both consistent quality and stable partner counterparts, the latter subject to staffing churn beyond RMBL's control.

The Critical-Path Stewardship Fund at $1.5M endowment provides meaningful but not comprehensive continuity protection. Some long-running programs may lapse over the period. This is a real tradeoff donors and staff should understand: Capacity funds transformation at the cost of less comprehensive continuity protection than Stewardship provides.

The scenario does not model: a federal funding catastrophe of a scale that overwhelms both endowment protection and partnership infrastructure; a wildfire that destroys Gothic facilities; significant damage to RMBL365 in Crested Butte (a separate-site risk profile from Gothic); AI-driven disruption that fundamentally changes the economics of small-nonprofit field stations. Any of these would require adaptation. Capacity's resilience against major shocks is somewhat lower than Stewardship's because its commitments are more operationally complex and its institutional capacity more stretched.

## Looking further out: 2040–2050

*Speculative. Lower resolution than the primary horizon.*

By the mid-2040s, the in-house catalytic capacity the campaign endowed continues operating as durable infrastructure. The data platforms, AI synthesis tools, and cyberinfrastructure RMBL operates — direct descendants of the Knowledge Commons-era work the flywheel built — are standard tooling on which basin and broader mountain-system research routinely depends. The Community Engagement Fund's translation work compounds across the period; identifiable management decisions across the Mountain West are traceable to basin science inputs through Fund-coordinated partnerships.

The basin's long records — protected at critical-path levels — continue through the period through a combination of fund support and grant work. The marmot record reaches its centennial around 2046 under conditions where succession was managed through resources less ample than Stewardship would have provided. The meadow-warming experiment passes 50 years of continuous run in 2041. Whether these continue to measure baseline ecosystems or document transformation depends on contingencies the campaign does not control.

The institutional independence the campaign endowed contributes through the period in ways that are less visible than the more concrete commitments but durable. As peer institutions navigate the political conditions of the 2040s, RMBL's pre-positioned independence reserves and the diversified funding base the campaign built contribute to its continued ability to host inquiry without restriction.

By 2050, whether the multi-emphasis institutional shape is sustainable through a second 15-year horizon depends on choices made in the 2037 execution-stress moment, on the success of the 2040 next-horizon framing, on the donor base's continued willingness to support the more complex operation Capacity requires, and on external trajectories. Capacity's contribution to whatever 2050 looks like is the in-house institutional capacity for transformation that, in the scenario's primary horizon, did not previously exist at the scale the campaign provides.
