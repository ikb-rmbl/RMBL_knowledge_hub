# Centennial Capacity

*A Future Scenario in the `centennial-2027` set. Target magnitude, innovation-leaning. v2.1 under [Future Scenarios Framework v0.6](../../Future_scenarios_framework.md).*

---

## Synopsis

Centennial Capacity assumes that the next fifteen years will be defined by two pressures at once: continued strain on federal science funding, and a rapid maturation of AI tools that can finally read a century of basin records at the scale they deserve. The campaign organizes near the upper end of what RMBL might raise around four priorities: protecting the marmot, meadow, and snowmelt records that anchor basin science; growing RMBL's data scientists, archivists, and AI capacity so those records become usable in new ways; deepening partnerships with the surrounding community, tribal nations, and water districts through the year-round RMBL365 building in Crested Butte; and building the financial reserves that let RMBL stay independent through funding shocks. About a third of the work is continuity stewardship; the rest builds new capacity. The scenario forgoes large new atmospheric and watershed instrumentation and broad mechanistic experimental platforms. It asks donors and the institution to invest in records, people, partnerships, and independence — quiet capacities that compound over decades.

### Campaign deliverables

- **primary.** Grow RMBL's in-house data science and AI capacity. Add staff who can build the tools that let researchers re-read a century of basin records — digitizing field notebooks, linking datasets across decades, and developing AI methods that work for ecological data. This builds directly on the pattern that produced the Knowledge Commons: outside grants fund new RMBL tools, those tools attract more researchers, and the institution carries the capacity forward.
- **primary.** Renovate RMBL365 in Crested Butte into a year-round home for community partnerships. Add workshop space, a small lab, and meeting rooms suited to working with tribal natural-resources offices, water districts, ranchers, and schools. The building already exists; this is about fitting it out for the work RMBL wants to do there over the next decade.
- **secondary.** Protect the long records. Steward the marmot demographic record as it approaches its centennial, the meadow phenology series, and the snowmelt observations. This means stable archival systems, observer-succession planning with the guest scientists who carry the records, and the data infrastructure that keeps them usable.
- **secondary.** Build financial reserves and the staff capacity to win and manage diverse grants. Less dependence on any single federal source. Savings that buffer RMBL against shocks. The governance and operating depth that let RMBL stay an independent place where researchers can pursue questions without restriction.
- **supporting.** Digitize the archive. Convert decades of field notebooks, photographs, datasheets, and specimen records into formats that AI tools and future researchers can actually use. This is the unglamorous work that makes the rest possible.

### Frontier support strategies

- **F.cont.1.** The campaign protects the records that make the basin globally distinctive. It funds the archival systems, the protocol stewardship, and the partnerships with guest scientists who carry the marmot, meadow, and snowmelt observations forward. The work is undramatic. It is also the source material everything else in the scenario draws on.
- **F.innov.3.** The campaign grows RMBL's data scientists and AI capacity, digitizes the archive, and builds the tools that let researchers ask questions a century of records can answer for the first time. This is the frontier where RMBL's own staff are most essential — the synthesis depends on systems the institution builds and maintains, not just on visiting researchers using them.
- **F.innov.4.** Renovating RMBL365 gives community partnerships a year-round home in Crested Butte. The campaign funds modest partnership-coordination staff who work with tribal natural-resources offices, water districts, county planners, and schools — bringing them into basin research as participants rather than audiences.
- **F.innov.5.** The campaign builds the savings and the diversified funding base that protect RMBL's independence through tight federal years. It funds the governance work and the grant-management capacity that let RMBL remain a place where guest scientists can pursue questions without external restriction — an attribute that has become more valuable as some peer institutions face new limits.

### Forgone

This scenario does not fund new atmospheric and watershed instrumentation at the scale SAIL established. The Earth-systems integration work continues through the guest scientists already doing it, but RMBL does not build a permanent successor platform. The scenario also does not fund new mechanistic experimental infrastructure — common gardens, factorial warming arrays, or the kinds of platforms that would let mechanism-focused researchers work at larger scale on basin grounds. Cross-mountain comparative partnerships with peer field stations remain informal rather than supported by dedicated infrastructure. And the scenario does not fund a research translation function at the scale that would let RMBL routinely brief federal land-management agencies on its long-term records; the agency-facing work happens through community partnerships and individual guest scientists, not through a dedicated program.

### Seeds in the present

The seeds are already in the ground. The marmot demographic record approaches its centennial. The meadow phenology series and snowmelt records are among the longest of their kind anywhere. The Knowledge Commons itself — this site — is a product of the pattern this scenario extends: outside grants built RMBL's data tools, which attracted more researchers, which funded the next round of capacity. RMBL's technical staff already includes a geospatial data scientist, a GIS manager, and instrumentation technicians. The 2025 purchase of the RMBL365 building in Crested Butte gave the organization a year-round venue for community work for the first time. Recent basin science — the Prather phenology synthesis, the Campbell floral-trait work, the Philson marmot social-structure papers, the Lu methylation clocks — points directly toward questions that AI-assisted analysis of long records can newly address. The current federal funding strain is the operating environment, not a forecast.

## Setting

This scenario assumes federal science funding stays tight through the late 2020s and recovers only partially by the mid-2030s. It assumes AI tools for ecological data mature steadily — not as a single breakthrough, but as a series of usable capacities that arrive year by year. And it assumes climate continues on a middle path: snowpack thins, foresummer drought deepens, the basin's records become more valuable each year as the signal of change strengthens.

Under these conditions, RMBL makes a particular bet. The most consequential thing the institution can do is protect what it already has — a century of records, an independent nonprofit charter, a small and capable technical staff — and grow the capacities that let those records be read in new ways. The campaign organizes near the upper end of what RMBL might realistically raise.

The work is split between four priorities. Stewardship of the long records continues, with careful succession planning and stable archival systems. RMBL's data scientists and AI capacity grow, so a century of field notebooks and datasheets can be digitized, linked, and analyzed at scales prior generations could not attempt. The RMBL365 building in Crested Butte is renovated into a year-round home for community partnerships — with tribal natural-resources offices, water districts, schools, and ranchers. And RMBL builds the financial reserves and grant-management staff that keep it independent through tight federal years.

The basin's atmospheric and watershed science continues through the guest scientists already doing it. But RMBL does not build a permanent successor to SAIL. The institution chooses depth over breadth.

## The arc — three phases across the primary horizon

### Phase 1: Foundations (2026–2030)

The early years are about hiring and building. By 2028 RMBL has added two data scientists and an archivist to the technical staff, alongside a community partnerships coordinator working out of RMBL365. The building renovation begins in 2027 — workshop space, a small lab, meeting rooms — and finishes in 2029. The archive digitization project starts the same year, with the marmot field notebooks and the meadow phenology datasheets as first priorities. The campaign launches publicly in 2027 around the centennial framing: a century of basin records, and the people and tools to read them. Early community partnerships take shape through RMBL365 — a working group with the Ute Indian Tribe on traditional plant knowledge, a water-quality monitoring partnership with the Upper Gunnison River Water Conservancy District, a teacher institute with Gunnison County schools. By 2030 the marmot record passes 65 continuous years and the meadow record approaches 50. Federal funding remains tight; the campaign's reserves component begins building the savings that will matter later. The first AI-assisted re-analysis of the long phenology records is published in 2030 by a guest scientist using tools RMBL's new data team built.

### Phase 2: Compounding (2031–2035)

The middle years are when the early investments start to compound. By 2033 the archive digitization is substantially complete: roughly 80 years of field notebooks, datasheets, photographs, and specimen records are now machine-readable. The data team has built AI tools that let researchers ask cross-decade questions in days rather than years. A wave of synthesis papers appears between 2032 and 2035 — re-reading the marmot record against climate, finding patterns in pollinator visitation across forty years, linking snowmelt timing to vegetation responses at scales the original observers could not see. Guest scientist applications rise as the analytical tools attract people who could not previously work at this depth. RMBL365 is now in steady use year-round: the community partnerships established in phase one mature into ongoing collaborations, with the water district and tribal partners co-authoring papers and shaping research questions. The reserves built in phase one prove their worth in 2034 when a federal grant cycle is disrupted; RMBL bridges affected guest scientists through the gap, and the experience becomes part of how donors understand what independence buys. By 2035 the marmot record passes 70 years.

### Phase 3: Centennial (2036–2040)

The final phase is shaped by the centennial itself. The marmot record reaches 100 continuous years in 2038 — a milestone almost no other study of any mammal anywhere has reached. RMBL marks it with a symposium and a synthesis volume that draws on the AI-assisted analyses of the prior years. The basin's records are now widely understood as global scientific infrastructure, and the institution's role in protecting them is part of how external funders and partners think about RMBL. The community partnerships through RMBL365 have moved from individual projects to a steady fabric of work; the building is in use most weeks of the year. The data team — now five people — supports a growing community of guest scientists doing retrospective work alongside the field-based research that continues. Federal funding has stabilized at a lower baseline than the early 2020s but is more predictable; the diversified base built through the campaign is doing real work. RMBL's independence is intact and visible. By 2040 the institution has chosen what to be for its second century: a steward of long records, a builder of analytical tools that let those records speak, and a year-round neighbor to the communities around it.

## Lines of inquiry

Phenology work matures into mechanism. With digitized records and AI tools, guest scientists can finally ask not just when wildflowers bloom but how the timing has shifted across individual plants, across genotypes, across microsites over decades. The Anderson 2012 plasticity-versus-evolution question gets revisited at scales the original work could only hint at. Marmot social-structure work, building on the Philson and Blumstein papers, gets linked to the full century of demographic records through methylation-based age estimation and AI-assisted behavioral analysis. The pollinator decline question — which Harvey and others have raised globally — gets a basin-grounded answer drawing on forty years of visitation records that were previously too laborious to fully analyze. Plant-pollinator network rewiring, the CaraDonna tradition, deepens through the same archive work. Cross-decade snowmelt and vegetation linkages emerge from the digitized meadow records. What does not mature is the atmosphere-to-bedrock integration work that SAIL began; that continues through the guest scientists doing it but does not become a permanent RMBL platform. Mechanistic experimental ecology — common gardens, factorial warming — continues at the scale guest scientists bring, not at the scale a new dedicated platform would enable. The community partnerships develop their own lines of inquiry: tribal plant knowledge integrated with basin botany, water-district questions about return flows and downstream timing, school-based phenology observations that extend the geographic reach of the records.

## Moments of choice within the primary horizon

1. **2027 — How to weight the campaign between endowment and capital deployment** *Shared inflection: `endowment-capital-split-2028`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and board
   - Alternatives: Heavier endowment to protect long-term independence, heavier capital to move quickly on staff and the RMBL365 renovation, or a balanced split
   - Scenario assumption: A balanced split that funds the immediate hires and the building renovation in the first five years while building reserves substantial enough to buffer at least one major federal funding cycle disruption

2. **2028 — How to scope the archive digitization project** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the technical staff
   - Alternatives: Digitize everything as comprehensively as possible, prioritize the longest and most-used records first, or build the tools and let guest scientists drive what gets digitized
   - Scenario assumption: Prioritize the longest continuous records first — marmot, meadow phenology, snowmelt — and build out from there as the data team grows

3. **2030 — What kind of community organization RMBL365 becomes** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and community partners
   - Alternatives: A venue that hosts partner-led work, a hub where RMBL convenes its own community programs, or a hybrid
   - Scenario assumption: A hybrid where RMBL hosts a small set of recurring partnerships — tribal, water district, schools — and makes space available for partner-led work alongside

4. **2032 — Whether to build AI tools as internal infrastructure or release them as open community resources** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the data science team
   - Alternatives: Keep tools internal to support RMBL-affiliated researchers, release everything openly to maximize reach, or release deliberately while keeping development capacity internal
   - Scenario assumption: Release deliberately. Tools are open and documented but RMBL keeps the development capacity that lets the tools keep improving with the science

5. **2034 — How to respond to a federal grant disruption that affects multiple guest scientists** *Shared inflection: `stewardship-priority-2032`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and board
   - Alternatives: Hold reserves for institutional needs, deploy bridging funds for affected researchers, or seek emergency external support
   - Scenario assumption: Deploy reserves to bridge affected guest scientists through the gap, demonstrating to donors and partners what the campaign's independence component actually buys

6. **2038 — How to mark the marmot record's centennial and what it signals about RMBL's next decades** *Shared inflection: `next-horizon-2040`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and the broader basin research community
   - Alternatives: A celebratory milestone that closes a chapter, a launch point for a renewed second-century commitment, or a quieter scientific event without strategic framing
   - Scenario assumption: A launch point. The centennial becomes the moment RMBL articulates its second-century identity as steward, builder, and neighbor

## Audience lens — research

If you are a scientist whose work depends on long records, this is a period when the basin becomes more useful, not less. The phenology series, the marmot record, the snowmelt observations — all are protected and increasingly machine-readable. You can ask questions that span decades in weeks. The data team at RMBL builds tools you can use and helps you adapt them to your own questions. If your work is more atmospheric or watershed-focused, the basin remains a place to work but RMBL is not building new permanent instrumentation; you bring your own platforms as guest scientists have always done. If you work in community-engaged or co-produced research, RMBL365 is a real venue with real partnerships you can plug into. The institution stays independent through the period, which matters if your questions might face restriction elsewhere.

## Audience lens — institution

RMBL becomes a slightly larger but still small institution — perhaps fifteen year-round staff by 2040, up from around ten now. The technical core grows most: data scientists, an archivist, an AI specialist, alongside the geospatial and instrumentation staff already in place. A community partnerships coordinator works out of RMBL365 year-round. The Crested Butte presence is real and steady, not seasonal. Donors and partners increasingly understand RMBL as a steward of records and a builder of tools, not only as a summer field station. The financial reserves built through the campaign make budget planning calmer than it has been in years. Federal grants still matter but no longer dominate the way they did. The institution feels less precarious and more confidently itself.

## Audience lens — donor

Your contribution joins a campaign organized around things that are hard to rebuild once lost. A century of continuous records. The independent nonprofit charter that lets researchers pursue questions without restriction. The small technical staff that makes the records usable. The year-round building in Crested Butte that connects RMBL to the people who live here. None of this is dramatic. All of it compounds. You are part of building the conditions under which the basin's second century of science can happen — with the records intact, the tools to read them growing, the partnerships with neighbors deepening, and the institution itself stable enough to plan in decades rather than grant cycles. Donors at every level are part of this work; the campaign succeeds because many people see why these quiet capacities matter.

## Overlay robustness

**Central case.** Federal funding stays tight through the late 2020s and partially recovers by the mid-2030s. AI tools for ecological data mature steadily across the period. Climate follows a middle path with thinning snowpack and deepening foresummer drought. Under these conditions the scenario plays out close to as described: the records are protected, the data team grows, RMBL365 becomes a year-round community hub, and the reserves do meaningful work during at least one federal disruption.

**Stress case — Deeper federal contraction.** If federal science funding contracts further rather than stabilizing — affecting NSF and DOE programs that currently support many basin guest scientists — the campaign's reserves component becomes more central than expected. RMBL bridges more researchers through more gaps. The community partnerships through RMBL365 become more important as a way of demonstrating RMBL's broader value to the region. The AI-assisted synthesis work continues because much of its source material is already in hand, but the rate of new field research slows. The institution stays solvent and independent but the surrounding research community is smaller.

**Stress case — Faster AI advance.** If AI tools advance faster than expected — particularly in extracting findings from unstructured field records — the archive digitization work becomes the rate-limiting step rather than the analysis. RMBL prioritizes digitization heavily in phase one and the synthesis wave arrives earlier, perhaps by 2032 rather than 2034. The data team grows faster but stays small. The basin's records become globally visible as a test case for what a century of ecological observation can yield when fully readable. Demand for guest scientist access rises.

**Stress case — Sharper climate signal.** If snowpack loss and drought intensify faster than the middle path assumes, the long records become more urgently valuable but the basin itself changes faster than they were designed to track. The protocol stewardship work has to adapt as some phenology measurements lose their original meaning under transformed conditions. Community partnerships through RMBL365 take on more weight as water districts and tribal partners face more acute decisions. The campaign's investments still hold but the questions the records are asked to answer shift toward what is being lost and how fast.

## Plausibility caveats

This scenario makes several assumptions readers should weigh. It assumes federal funding strain continues but does not collapse — a deeper contraction would force harder tradeoffs than the scenario describes. It assumes AI tools for ecological data continue to improve in usable ways; a stall or a reorientation of the field toward applications irrelevant to long records would undermine the data team's central role. It assumes RMBL can hire and retain technical staff in a competitive market, which gets harder as private-sector demand for data scientists stays strong. It assumes the community partnerships through RMBL365 develop as designed; partnerships are slow and depend on trust that cannot be scheduled.

The structural blind spots are real. The scenario emphasizes records and analysis over new mechanistic work, which means if the most important basin questions over the next fifteen years turn out to require experimental platforms the campaign does not fund, RMBL will not be positioned for them. It assumes the marmot study, the meadow phenology series, and the snowmelt records continue to have the guest scientists needed to carry them; observer succession is harder than budgets suggest, and a single retirement without a clear successor can interrupt a record permanently. It assumes RMBL365 renovation costs and timelines hold; construction in mountain communities often surprises. And the scenario does not fund the atmospheric and watershed instrumentation that has driven much of the basin's recent visibility — if SAIL's successor work falters without RMBL support, the basin's profile as integrated Earth-system science could narrow.

## Looking further out: 2040–2050

*Speculative. Lower resolution than the primary horizon.*

Looking past 2040, the scenario's bets either compound or they don't. If they compound, RMBL enters its second century as a place defined by records of unusual length, tools that make them readable, and a steady presence in the community that surrounds the field site. The marmot record at 110 years, the meadow phenology at 70, the snowmelt observations at similar depth — these become the empirical backbone of a generation of mountain ecology globally. The data team established in this campaign becomes a permanent feature of RMBL, and its tools are used across other long-term ecological sites. RMBL365 is a recognizable institution in Crested Butte. The reserves built through the campaign keep the organization independent through whatever the 2040s bring.

If the bets do not compound — if AI tools turn out less useful for this kind of data than expected, if the community partnerships do not deepen, if federal funding never stabilizes — RMBL is still an institution with intact records, a small capable staff, and a building it owns outright. That is not a failure mode. It is a foundation the next campaign starts from. The further future is hazier than the primary horizon, and the scenario does not try to resolve it.
