# Centennial Records and Independence

*A Future Scenario in the `centennial-2027` set. Floor magnitude, continuity-leaning. v1.0 under [Future Scenarios Framework v0.7](../../Future_scenarios_framework.md).*

---

## Synopsis

Centennial Records and Independence assumes the campaign comes in modest, and that RMBL responds by anchoring its work to two things that compound across decades: the basin's longest continuous records, and the institutional independence that protects them. The marmot study passes its seventy-fifth year during this period. The long meadow phenology series and snowmelt-driven plant work continue without lapse. Around those records, the campaign builds the savings, governance, and broader funding base that let RMBL keep doing this work whatever happens in Washington. About seventy percent of the work is continuity stewardship; the rest builds the financial and institutional buffers that protect it. The scenario forgoes new atmospheric instrumentation, AI synthesis programs, and broad community co-production work. It asks donors and the institution to invest in something quieter than expansion: the conditions under which a nonprofit field station can keep asking the questions it wants to ask, on the timescales the science actually requires.

### Campaign deliverables

- **primary.** Stewardship of the basin's longest records — the marmot demographic study now in its eighth decade, the meadow phenology and snowmelt series, and the plot infrastructure they depend on. Funds the protocols, archival systems, and the small technical core that supports the guest scientists carrying the records forward through the period.
- **primary.** Financial reserves that buffer RMBL against funding shocks. The campaign builds savings substantial enough that a one-year or two-year disruption to federal grants does not force RMBL to lay off technical staff, suspend record-collection, or compromise its independence in pursuit of restricted funding. This is the distinctive bet of the scenario — that durability of the records depends on durability of the institution holding them.
- **secondary.** Governance and development capacity for a less federally-dependent funding base. Modest investment in the staff and systems that let RMBL cultivate foundations, individual donors, and institutional partnerships at the scale a year-round nonprofit field station now needs. This is the scenario's other distinctive commitment: building the fundraising muscle that independence requires, rather than assuming the next campaign will repeat itself.
- **supporting.** Targeted renovation of RMBL365 in Crested Butte to support year-round operations and a stable in-town presence. Scope sized to what the campaign floor supports — winterized work and meeting space rather than major laboratory build-out.
- **continuity_residual.** Maintenance of the existing technical core (geospatial data scientist, GIS manager, instrumentation technicians) at current scale, with the data systems, archival capacity, and Knowledge Commons that the recent pattern of outside grants has already built.

### Frontier support strategies

- **F.cont.1.** The campaign protects the basin's longest records as the central work of the period. It funds protocol stewardship, plot infrastructure, archival systems, and the small technical staff who support the guest scientists running the marmot, meadow, and snowmelt studies. Because the campaign is modest, this support is narrow and durable rather than expansive — the goal is no lapse, not new instrumentation.
- **F.innov.5.** The campaign treats RMBL's nonprofit independence as a strategic asset and invests in what protects it: financial reserves that buffer funding shocks, governance and fundraising capacity that reduces dependence on any single source, and the year-round in-town presence that makes RMBL visible to a broader donor and partner base. The scenario's bet is that these institutional investments are what let the records of frontier one survive a constrained federal period.

### Forgone

This scenario does not fund new atmospheric or watershed instrumentation. The atmospheric and subsurface integration work that the SAIL campaign opened continues only through external grants its guest-scientist community wins on its own. It does not fund a data-science and AI synthesis program large enough to re-read the century of basin records at the scales now possible; that work waits for a later campaign or for grant-funded growth. It does not fund broad community co-production work with tribal nations, water districts, or local stakeholder groups beyond what existing staff can sustain. It does not fund cross-mountain comparative partnerships or new mechanistic experimental platforms. The campaign deliberately stays narrow. The bet is that protecting the records and the institution that holds them matters more, at this magnitude, than spreading thin across frontiers that need more capital than the campaign can offer.

### Seeds in the present

The marmot demographic record, now in its eighth decade by the campaign horizon, is the clearest seed (Philson and Blumstein, 2023; Van de Walle et al., 2023). So is the long meadow phenology series and the snowmelt-driven plant work that the basin has carried since the 1970s (Inouye, 2022; Campbell et al., 2021). The RMBL365 building in Crested Butte, purchased in 2025, gives the scenario a year-round in-town platform that did not exist a decade ago. The recent pattern of federal funding disruption is the current operating environment, not a future risk — researchers associated with RMBL have already felt it. The Knowledge Commons itself, built through the recent pattern where outside grants fund RMBL's own tools, is the kind of in-house capacity the campaign now needs to maintain rather than expand. And the planning theme of long-term integrated records as the load-bearing observational substrate of basin science recurs across nearly every cross-lens analysis.

## Setting

This scenario assumes the campaign comes in modest. The donor base supports a focused effort rather than a transformative one, and RMBL chooses to spend that effort on what it judges most durable: the basin's longest records, and the institutional independence that protects them.

Federal funding stays constrained through most of the period. Some recovery is possible in the late 2030s, but the campaign does not assume it. RMBL's guest-scientist community continues to win grants, but at lower rates than a decade ago. The institutions that house many of those scientists face their own pressures; a few face direct restrictions on what they can study. Against that backdrop, RMBL's nonprofit independence becomes an increasingly visible asset — the kind of place where a researcher can still ask whatever question the science requires.

The campaign organizes at the campaign floor around two priorities. First: stewardship of the long records — the marmot study, the meadow phenology series, the snowmelt-driven plant work — through protocols, plots, archives, and the small technical staff who support the guest scientists carrying them forward. Second: the savings, governance, and broader funding base that let RMBL absorb a federal shock without compromising the records or the freedom to study them.

What the scenario does not attempt is also part of its shape. No new atmospheric instrumentation. No major AI synthesis program. No broad community co-production initiative. The campaign chooses depth over breadth, and durability over reach. Its working hypothesis is that records that have survived a century are worth the institutional effort it takes to keep them intact through a constrained decade.

## The arc — three phases across the primary horizon

### Phase 1: Anchoring the records, building the reserves (2026–2030)

The campaign launches and closes its early phase by 2028. RMBL leadership and board commit to a narrow scenario: stewardship of the long records and the financial reserves that protect them. The marmot study, the meadow phenology work, and the snowmelt series continue without interruption. The small technical core — the geospatial data scientist, the GIS manager, the instrumentation technicians — stays intact through the federal contraction, paid in part from new reserves rather than from grant cycles. RMBL365 receives a modest first round of renovation: winterized meeting and work space, no laboratory build-out yet. By 2030 the financial buffer is large enough that a single bad grant year does not force layoffs or program suspensions, and a modest development team has begun cultivating a broader base of foundation and individual donors. Guest scientists begin to mention RMBL's independence explicitly when asked why they bring their work to the basin.

### Phase 2: Continuity through a constrained period (2031–2035)

Federal funding remains tight. Several partner institutions face restrictions on what their researchers may study. RMBL's independence becomes a recurring topic in recruitment conversations; a handful of established research groups relocate parts of their fieldwork to the basin specifically because the institutional posture is unrestricted. The marmot study passes its seventy-fifth year in 2038 — a milestone marked carefully, without the campaign-style expansion that a different scenario might have built around it. The meadow and snowmelt records continue. A guest scientist wins a major grant to digitize a portion of the historical field notebooks; RMBL hosts the work but does not fund it. The reserves are tested once during the period, when a federal program ends abruptly mid-year, and the buffer absorbs the shock. The development team grows from one person to two.

### Phase 3: A quieter institution, intact (2036–2040)

By the late 2030s, the federal landscape has neither collapsed nor recovered. RMBL has spent the period being a smaller, quieter, more focused version of itself than the upper-bracket scenarios imagined. The records are intact. The marmot study has continued past its seventy-fifth year. The meadow phenology and snowmelt series have continued without lapse. The technical core is the same size as in 2026, but more secure. The reserves are substantial enough to plan multi-year stewardship commitments rather than year-by-year ones. The guest-scientist community has become measurably more diverse in its funding sources, partly because RMBL's broader development work has connected researchers to foundations they would not otherwise have known. The institution closes the period with a clearer sense of what its independence is for, and a credible case to make to the next campaign that records and durability are the foundation everything else builds on.

## Lines of inquiry

The research that matures through the period is the research the long records make possible. The marmot study passes its seventy-fifth year and begins to support questions that only a century of individually-resolved data can ask — about how social structure, demography, and individual life histories interact across multiple climate regimes. The meadow phenology and snowmelt-driven plant work continues to deepen, feeding broader syntheses on mismatch, plasticity, and the long arc of climate response. Guest scientists continue to win grants on these systems, and several use the period to publish the kinds of multi-decadal analyses that shorter records cannot support. What does not mature, in this scenario, is the atmospheric and subsurface integration work that the SAIL campaign opened — that continues through external grants the basin's hydrology community wins on its own, without new RMBL-built instrumentation. AI-assisted retrospective synthesis of the basin's archival records waits for later capacity. Community co-production with tribal nations and water districts proceeds at the pace existing staff can sustain, not as a campaign-funded program. Cross-mountain comparative partnerships develop opportunistically rather than through dedicated investment. The scenario's working bet is that depth in the records is the most durable contribution RMBL can make at this magnitude.

## Moments of choice within the primary horizon

1. **2028 — How to split the campaign between immediate capital projects and long-term financial reserves** *Shared inflection: `endowment-capital-split-2028`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and development team
   - Alternatives: The campaign could direct most funds to renovation, equipment, and near-term staffing — visible deliverables that show donors what their gifts built. Or it could direct a substantial share to reserves, accepting that the campaign closes with less to point at but more to weather with.
   - Scenario assumption: Reserves receive an unusually large share for a campaign of this size — a choice the development team frames as protecting the records rather than building new ones.

2. **2029 — Whether to articulate institutional independence as an explicit campaign rationale** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and board
   - Alternatives: RMBL could keep its independence framing implicit, leaving it as background context. Or it could make independence an explicit part of the campaign's case for support — useful for donors who care about it, but a register some donors find uncomfortable.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL articulates independence explicitly, in non-partisan terms, as part of what the campaign is protecting. The framing turns out to resonate with a meaningful segment of the donor base.

3. **2032 — Which long records receive the most stewardship investment as the campaign settles into operations** *Shared inflection: `stewardship-priority-2032`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the technical core
   - Alternatives: Resources could be spread evenly across the marmot, meadow, and snowmelt records. Or they could concentrate on the most fragile or most milestone-anchored of them, accepting that other records receive only baseline support.
   - Scenario assumption: Resources concentrate on the marmot study as it passes its seventy-fifth year, on the meadow phenology series, and on the snowmelt work — the three records the campaign identifies as most consequential and most fragile to lapse.

4. **2033 — Whether to expand fundraising capacity mid-campaign in response to continued federal contraction** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, development team, and board
   - Alternatives: RMBL could hold the development team at its post-campaign size and accept that fundraising capacity will plateau. Or it could grow the team further, accepting that the growth itself draws on reserves until it pays back.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL grows the development team modestly — from one to two — accepting the short-term cost as part of what independence requires over a longer horizon.

5. **2036 — Whether to redirect reserves toward a new initiative when federal funding shows partial signs of recovery** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and guest-scientist community
   - Alternatives: Partial federal recovery could be read as permission to spend down reserves on a long-deferred initiative — atmospheric instrumentation, AI synthesis, community co-production. Or the reserves could be held as durable buffer regardless of short-term signals.
   - Scenario assumption: Reserves are held. RMBL judges the recovery too uncertain to spend against, and chooses to protect the records and the institution rather than reach for the deferred frontiers.

6. **2040 — How to frame the institution to the campaign that follows this one** *Shared inflection: `next-horizon-2040`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and the broader research community
   - Alternatives: The next campaign could be pitched as expansion — atmospheric integration, AI synthesis, community co-production all queued up. Or it could be pitched as the deepening of the records-and-independence work this campaign began.
   - Scenario assumption: The next campaign is positioned as expansion built on the records-and-independence foundation this campaign secured — a sequencing argument rather than a course correction.

## Audience lens — research

If you bring your research to the basin during this period, you find an institution that has chosen to be a little smaller and a little more secure than the more ambitious scenarios imagined. The long records you might want to work with — the marmots, the meadow phenology, the snowmelt-driven plant work — are intact and stewarded carefully. The technical staff who maintain the data systems and the plot infrastructure are still there, and more securely employed than they were five years ago. What you do not find is new atmospheric instrumentation, a new AI synthesis program, or a major community co-production initiative. If those are central to your work, you bring your own grants for them. What RMBL offers is the kind of place where you can ask whatever question your science requires, on a basin whose records remain the deepest you can work with.

## Audience lens — institution

RMBL becomes, through this period, a smaller and more durable version of itself. The technical core is the same size in 2040 as in 2026, but the people in those roles can plan their work in multi-year horizons rather than grant-cycle ones. The reserves are substantial enough to absorb a bad year without layoffs. The development team is twice the size it was at the start, and the funding base is meaningfully less dependent on any single source. RMBL365 is a year-round working presence in Crested Butte, modestly renovated. The Gothic site looks much as it did, with the long records intact and the small staff who support them stably employed. What RMBL feels like to work at is a place that has chosen depth over breadth, and that has been honest with its community about why.

## Audience lens — donor

Your contribution joins a deliberately focused effort. The campaign is not the most ambitious version of RMBL's future; it is the version that protects what is hardest to rebuild — a century of marmot demography, decades of meadow phenology, the snowmelt-driven plant work that has shaped basin ecology — through a period when the federal support these records have leaned on cannot be assumed. Your gift is also part of building the financial buffer and the broader donor base that let RMBL keep its nonprofit independence intact, at a moment when independence is becoming rarer in the research landscape. This is an invitation to support something quieter than expansion: the conditions under which a small field station can keep doing the work it judges most consequential, on the timescales the science actually requires. The records continue because someone chose to make their continuation possible.

## Overlay robustness

**Central case.** Under gradual federal contraction, gradual AI evolution, and a moderate climate trajectory, the scenario plays close to its baseline. The records continue. The reserves are tested but not exhausted. The development team grows modestly and broadens the funding base. AI tools for retrospective synthesis become more capable through the period, but RMBL does not invest in deploying them at scale — the work waits, and the records sit ready for a later campaign or a guest-scientist project to take up. RMBL's independence becomes a more visible part of its identity. The marmot study passes its seventy-fifth year in a constrained but intact institution.

**Stress case — Sharper federal contraction.** If federal funding contracts more sharply — agency reorganizations, multi-year program suspensions — the reserves do the work the scenario was designed for. They absorb the shock, the technical core stays intact, and the long records continue. The development team accelerates its diversification work. The scenario plays harder but its central bet pays out more clearly: institutional independence and financial buffer are exactly what carries the records through. Guest scientists displaced from constrained institutions look to RMBL more actively, and the institution gains a small but durable reputational advantage.

**Stress case — Partial federal recovery in the mid-2030s.** If federal science funding partially recovers, the scenario faces a temptation it largely resists. New grant opportunities open. The guest-scientist community wins more of them. RMBL holds its reserves rather than spending them on the deferred frontiers, judging the recovery too uncertain. By 2040 some critics argue the scenario was too cautious — that atmospheric instrumentation or AI synthesis could have launched mid-period. The defenders argue that durability of the records was worth the foregone reach. The next campaign inherits the debate.

**Stress case — Faster climate change.** If climate change moves faster than the central case — more severe snowpack loss, more frequent ecological thresholds crossed — the long records become more scientifically valuable, not less. They become the baselines against which transformation is measured. The scenario's bet on continuity looks stronger in retrospect. What the scenario does not have is the new infrastructure to study the transformed systems in real time; that gap is felt, but the records that make the gap visible are themselves intact.

## Plausibility caveats

The scenario assumes that RMBL's donor base will support a campaign whose visible deliverables are mostly defensive — records protected, reserves built, independence preserved — rather than expansive. This is the scenario's most distinctive failure mode. Capital campaigns at the floor magnitude have historically been easier to close around visible new things than around durability of existing things. If donors prove unwilling to fund stewardship at scale, the campaign closes lower than the floor and the scenario does not happen as written.

The scenario also assumes that federal contraction stays roughly within the range the reserves were sized for. A faster or deeper collapse would test the buffer harder than the scenario imagines; a fuller recovery would make the conservative posture look like missed opportunity. Both are within the range of plausible outcomes, and the scenario's bet is that the median outcome is closer to its central case than to either extreme.

The scenario assumes RMBL's guest-scientist community continues to win grants at meaningful rates, even at a lower rate than a decade ago. If the guest-scientist model itself comes under strain — if academic institutions stop sending researchers, or if the grant base for ecological field science contracts faster than expected — the records remain intact in principle but increasingly thin in practice.

The structural blind spot worth naming is opportunity cost across the scenario set. By forgoing atmospheric instrumentation, AI synthesis, and community co-production, the scenario depends on those frontiers either waiting patiently for a later campaign or being advanced by others. If the basin's atmospheric work decisively moves elsewhere during the period — to a peer institution, a federal lab, a different field station — RMBL's relevance in that domain may be harder to recover than the scenario assumes.

## Looking further out: 2040–2050

*Speculative. Lower resolution than the primary horizon.*

By 2050, the shape of the period is clearer. The records survived. The marmot study has continued past its seventy-fifth year with continuous data. The meadow phenology and snowmelt series, now near sixty years long, have become reference datasets that researchers around the world depend on. RMBL's nonprofit independence, defended through a constrained decade, has become part of how the institution is recognized in the broader research landscape — a place where independent inquiry continued when independence elsewhere narrowed.

The deferred frontiers remain deferred, but not forgotten. Atmospheric integration has matured somewhere — at RMBL through grants its guest-scientist community has won, or at peer institutions, or both. AI-assisted retrospective synthesis of the basin's records has become possible for guest scientists who bring the capacity, even though RMBL itself did not build it. The next campaign, whenever it comes, builds on a smaller, intact, durable foundation rather than a larger and more fragile one. Whether that was the right bet is the kind of question a later generation will judge with the records the scenario chose to protect.
