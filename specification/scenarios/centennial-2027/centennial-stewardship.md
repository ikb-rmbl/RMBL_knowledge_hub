# Centennial Stewardship

*A Future Scenario in the `centennial-2027` set. Target magnitude, continuity-leaning. v2.0 under [Future Scenarios Framework v0.6](../../Future_scenarios_framework.md).*

---

## Synopsis

Centennial Stewardship takes as its central contingency the recognition that the basin's longest ecological records — the marmot demographic study approaching its centennial, the meadow phenology series, the snowmelt-driven plant demography work — represent irreplaceable global infrastructure whose continuity through the next decade is not guaranteed under federal contraction. The campaign organizes near the upper end of the realistic bracket around protecting that continuity while building the in-house data and AI capacity that lets a century of accumulated basin records yield findings prior generations could not extract. The portfolio leans continuity-heavy (roughly two-thirds), pairing classical archival and observational stewardship with growth of RMBL's geospatial and data-science core and with the governance and operating reserves that make institutional independence a durable platform for guest-scientist inquiry. What this scenario forgoes is breadth: it does not significantly fund new atmospheric or watershed integration infrastructure beyond what SAIL-era partnerships already provide, does not expand community-facing programs beyond current RMBL365 functions, and does not build dedicated translation capacity for water-management decisions. The scenario asks donors and the institution to commit to a posture of stewardship — to treat the basin's century-long records as the rare global asset they are, to invest in the in-house capacity that makes those records newly legible, and to accept that durable protection of a few load-bearing things is a more honest ambition than visible expansion across many.

### Campaign deliverables

- **primary.** Archival, protocol-stewardship, and observer-succession infrastructure for the basin's longest continuous records — marmot demography, meadow phenology and demography, snowmelt-driven plant series. Includes physical archive conditions, digitization of legacy field records, metadata systems, and the partnership-coordination capacity that sustains guest-scientist succession across generations of investigators.
- **primary.** Growth of in-house data-science, geospatial, and AI-methods capacity, building on the technical core that the innovation-to-infrastructure flywheel has matured. This is the load-bearing capacity for retrospective synthesis of long basin records and for the Knowledge Commons platforms that make those records discoverable and analyzable.
- **secondary.** Operating reserves and governance capacity that protect institutional independence — diversified-funding-base infrastructure, reserves sized to absorb federal-funding volatility without compromising research direction, and the institutional-capacity investments that let RMBL continue winning and executing external grants that feed the flywheel.
- **supporting.** Instrumentation continuity for the long-record studies — replacement and modernization of monitoring equipment, calibration infrastructure, and the technician capacity that keeps decades-old observation protocols comparable across instrument generations.
- **continuity_residual.** Modest renovation and outfitting at the existing RMBL365 facility to support year-round archival access, data-science work, and visiting-scholar staging for retrospective synthesis projects that do not require the seasonal Gothic site.

### Frontier support strategies

- **F.cont.1.** Direct primary investment in the archival, protocol, and observer-succession infrastructure that makes centennial-scale observation possible. The campaign treats the long records as global infrastructure — comparable in significance to a long-running atmospheric baseline station — and funds the unglamorous continuity capacity (cold storage for specimens, digitization of paper field books, protocol documentation, succession partnerships with guest scientists) that no single grant cycle will sustain.
- **F.cont.2.** Targeted support for marmot-record continuity through its centennial — instrumentation continuity, archival access for the molecular and demographic streams now converging on the record, and the field infrastructure that the guest-scientist team carrying the record depends on. RMBL's role remains catalytic; the science is led by the long-running guest-investigator program.
- **F.innov.3.** Build the in-house data-science and AI-methods capacity that makes a century of accumulated records newly analyzable. This is where in-house RMBL capacity is genuinely load-bearing: archival digitization, structured-data extraction from historical records, AI-assisted pattern detection across multi-decade series, and methodological partnerships with guest scientists. Depends on and reinforces F.cont.1 — the synthesis is only possible because the records exist.
- **F.innov.5.** Fund the governance, reserves, and diversified-funding-base capacity that sustain institutional independence as a platform for inquiry. This frontier is served less by new physical infrastructure than by the operating reserves and institutional capacity that let RMBL maintain unrestricted research direction across funding cycles, and by the facility quality that keeps the basin attractive to guest scientists seeking independent inquiry venues.

### Forgone

Centennial Stewardship deliberately does not fund several things a more expansive portfolio might. It does not build new atmosphere-to-bedrock integration infrastructure beyond what SAIL-era partnerships already provide — the integrative Earth-systems frontier is left to continue under existing federal and partnership arrangements, with the risk that contraction in those arrangements is not buffered by this campaign. It does not expand mountain-water-security translation capacity; the regional water-management interfaces that planning themes identified as stakeholder-pull opportunities remain underbuilt at campaign close. It does not significantly grow community-science partnerships or co-production infrastructure beyond current RMBL365 functions — the community-engagement dimension is sustained but not advanced. It does not fund cross-mountain comparative partnership infrastructure that would position the basin within a network of peer mountain observatories. And it accepts that some classical infrastructure renewal at the Gothic site (housing, lab modernization, utilities) is deferred to future campaigns. The tradeoff is honest: this scenario protects the rare global asset that the long records represent, and builds the in-house capacity to extract their accumulated meaning, at the cost of visible expansion across the other frontiers a centennial campaign could pursue.

### Seeds in the present

The scenario rests on capacity that already exists in 2026. The marmot demographic record is approaching its centennial under continuous guest-scientist stewardship. The meadow phenology and demography series — anchored in the work synthesized by Inouye (2022) and the mechanistic phenology lineage running from Anderson et al. (2012) through Campbell et al. (2021) and Prather et al. (2023) — provides the half-century empirical backbone the synthesis frontier requires. RMBL's technical core (geospatial data scientist, GIS manager, instrumentation technicians as of 2026) is the seed of the in-house capacity this scenario grows. The Knowledge Commons itself is a product of the innovation-to-infrastructure flywheel and is the platform on which AI-assisted retrospective synthesis becomes tractable. RMBL365, acquired in 2025, provides the year-round in-town facility that archival and data-science work can occupy without seasonal constraint. The planning-theme convergence on integrated long-term records (themes 6, 9, 10) and on the need for connective infrastructure rather than new descriptive programs (themes 2, 4) is the cross-lens signal this scenario takes most seriously.

## Setting

This scenario takes as its central contingency that the basin's longest continuous ecological records — the marmot demographic study, the meadow phenology and demography series, the snowmelt-driven plant work — constitute global infrastructure whose continuity through the next fifteen years is genuinely at risk under sustained federal contraction, and that the institutional response most aligned with RMBL's catalytic mission is to protect that continuity while building the in-house capacity that makes a century of accumulated records newly legible.

The scenario organizes its campaign near the upper end of the realistic bracket and accepts a strong continuity tilt — roughly two-thirds continuity, one-third innovation — as the honest expression of its central judgment. The innovation share is concentrated in a single load-bearing investment: the growth of in-house data-science, geospatial, and AI-methods capacity that turns the long records into a synthesis platform. The continuity share is spread across archival systems, protocol stewardship, instrumentation continuity, and the governance and operating reserves that protect institutional independence as the platform on which guest-scientist inquiry continues. What gets forgone is breadth — the atmospheric integration, water translation, and community-partnership frontiers that a more expansive portfolio would advance. What gets protected is a rare global asset and the capacity to extract its accumulated meaning.

## The arc — three phases across the primary horizon

### Phase 1: Stabilization and archival commitment (2026–2030)

The campaign's early years concentrate on stabilizing the long-record programs against federal-funding volatility and on the unglamorous archival work that makes everything downstream possible. Digitization of legacy field records — paper field books, specimen catalogs, historical phenology sheets — proceeds at sustained pace through partnerships with the guest scientists who hold institutional memory. Protocol documentation captures the tacit knowledge of long-running studies before it is lost to investigator transitions. Operating reserves are built toward levels that let RMBL absorb a multi-year federal shortfall without compromising the long-record programs. Initial growth of the in-house data-science core adds capacity for metadata systems, structured-data extraction, and the cyberinfrastructure foundations on which later AI-methods work will run. RMBL365 receives the modest renovations needed to host year-round archival and data-science functions. By 2030 the institutional posture has shifted: the long records are no longer dependent on the funding cycle of any single grant, and the in-house capacity to work with them is materially larger than at campaign start.

### Phase 2: Synthesis capacity matures (2031–2035)

The middle phase is when in-house AI-methods and data-science capacity reaches working scale and the retrospective synthesis frontier becomes genuinely active. Methodological partnerships with guest scientists produce the first wave of analyses that draw on multi-decade records at scales prior generations could not attempt — phenology-mismatch resolution across the full meadow series, marmot social-structure analyses that fuse the demographic record with methylation-age data and molecular streams, snowmelt-driven plant demography analyses that integrate basin records with SAIL-era atmospheric measurements. The marmot record passes its centennial mark and is recognized externally as one of the longest continuous mammal studies on the continent. The Knowledge Commons platform matures into a primary discovery layer for the basin's accumulated work. Institutional independence holds: diversified funding sources, including foundation and individual-donor support cultivated by the campaign, carry weight that federal sources cannot be counted on to provide. Guest scientists seeking unrestricted inquiry venues continue to choose RMBL as their basin platform.

### Phase 3: Centennial stewardship as institutional identity (2036–2040)

By the late 2030s the stewardship posture has become RMBL's institutional identity in a way it was not before. The basin's long records are referenced in global syntheses of climate-driven ecological change as among the few records long enough to resolve the questions being asked, and the in-house capacity that makes them analyzable is part of what RMBL is known for. Several major retrospective syntheses, produced through guest-scientist partnerships with RMBL's data-science core, reach external audiences. The campaign closes with the long-record programs durably protected against a further decade of federal-funding uncertainty and with the in-house capacity to support them at materially larger scale than in 2026. What the institution has not done — built new watershed-integration infrastructure, expanded community-partnership programs, grown a translation function for regional water management — is visible as the honest cost of the stewardship choice. The next campaign, in the early 2040s, can engage those frontiers from a foundation that the long-record protection has secured.

## Lines of inquiry

The research themes that mature through this horizon are those the long records were always going to enable, finally pursued at scales the data require. Phenology-mismatch resolution moves from descriptive documentation toward mechanistic and evolutionary inference across the full meadow series, drawing on the lineage running from Inouye through Anderson, Campbell, Powers, Navarro, and Prather. The marmot record, fused with molecular age-clock data and decades of behavioral observation, enables the social-ecology and life-history analyses that Philson, Blumstein, and collaborators have been building toward. Subalpine plant demography under snowmelt change yields multi-decadal cohort analyses that short studies cannot produce. AI-assisted synthesis extracts patterns from accumulated records — especially from the digitized legacy material — that prior analytical approaches could not access. What does not mature in this scenario: the atmosphere-to-bedrock integration frontier continues at the level existing partnerships sustain rather than being deepened by RMBL investment; community-co-production research does not significantly expand; cross-mountain comparative work proceeds through existing collaborations rather than new partnership infrastructure. The basin's plant-pollinator network research, while sustained by the broader long-record protection, is not separately prioritized as it would be in a portfolio that included F.cont.4.

## Moments of choice within the primary horizon

1. **2028 — How to split the campaign's reserves-and-endowment versus capital-and-staffing allocations given a stewardship-heavy portfolio.** *Shared inflection: `endowment-capital-split-2028`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and Development & Advancement team
   - Alternatives: Tilt toward endowment and operating reserves to protect long-record continuity across decades; or tilt toward capital and immediate in-house staffing growth to accelerate synthesis capacity now; or attempt a balanced split that achieves neither fully.
   - Scenario assumption: Leadership tilts moderately toward reserves and endowment-style vehicles, reasoning that durable protection of the long records is the campaign's central commitment and that synthesis capacity can be grown at a sustainable pace.

2. **2030 — Whether to hire dedicated AI-methods staff as part of the in-house core or to rely on methodological partnerships with guest scientists for AI capacity.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL technical leadership and Development team
   - Alternatives: Hire one or two AI-methods specialists as permanent RMBL staff, building in-house capacity at higher fixed cost; rely on guest-scientist partnerships and short-term postdoctoral arrangements; or pursue a hybrid with shared appointments.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL hires a small in-house AI-methods capacity (one to two FTE) as a durable extension of the technical core, on the judgment that load-bearing synthesis capacity should not be entirely contingent on external personnel cycles.

3. **2032 — Which stewardship priorities receive sustained investment as mid-campaign tradeoffs sharpen.** *Shared inflection: `stewardship-priority-2032`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and program directors
   - Alternatives: Concentrate continuing investment on the marmot and phenology long records; broaden to include plant-pollinator network continuity at the cost of depth elsewhere; or shift partial investment toward emerging snow-ecology baselines that the SAIL era has opened.
   - Scenario assumption: Leadership concentrates on the longest records (marmot, phenology, plant demography) and accepts that other continuity priorities are sustained at baseline rather than advanced.

4. **2034 — Whether to formalize observer-succession arrangements for the marmot record as its centennial approaches and the founding investigator generation transitions.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and guest-scientist community
   - Alternatives: Formalize succession through a multi-institutional partnership agreement; rely on informal continuity through existing collaborators; or restructure the project as a more directly RMBL-stewarded program with different institutional arrangements.
   - Scenario assumption: Leadership formalizes a multi-institutional succession partnership that preserves the guest-scientist research model while ensuring institutional continuity across investigator generations.

5. **2036 — Whether to use mid-to-late campaign capacity to broaden into a forgone frontier (water-management translation or community partnerships) or to deepen the stewardship and synthesis commitments.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and Development team
   - Alternatives: Open a modest water-translation or community-partnership line within remaining campaign capacity; deepen instead the AI-synthesis and archival commitments; or hold the line and direct surplus into reserves.
   - Scenario assumption: Leadership deepens the stewardship and synthesis commitments rather than broadening, maintaining the scenario's stewardship identity through campaign close.

6. **2039 — How to frame the next-horizon strategic plan given the stewardship campaign's near-close and the frontiers it has forgone.** *Shared inflection: `next-horizon-2040`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and strategic-planning function
   - Alternatives: Position the next campaign explicitly around the forgone frontiers (integration, translation, community partnerships); position it around extension of stewardship into newer long-record programs; or position it as a broader balanced portfolio that the secured foundation enables.
   - Scenario assumption: Leadership positions the next-horizon plan around the forgone integration and translation frontiers, on the explicit judgment that the stewardship foundation now secured makes a broader next campaign responsible rather than overreaching.

## Audience lens — research

For guest scientists working on the basin's long records and on retrospective synthesis, this horizon is one in which the unglamorous infrastructure they have always depended on becomes durably protected and the analytical capacity they have always wished for becomes available. Marmot researchers find that the demographic record they inherit is more completely archived, better documented, and more readily fused with molecular and behavioral data streams than it was a decade earlier. Phenology and plant-demography researchers find that fifty years of meadow records are digitized, structured, and analyzable at scales descriptive papers could not attempt. AI-methods collaborations with RMBL's in-house data-science core open mechanistic and synthesis analyses that depend on data integration the institution maintains. What researchers working on watershed integration, community co-production, or new atmospheric instrumentation find is that this campaign does not advance their frontiers — they continue to work through existing federal partnerships and existing collaborative arrangements, with the basin itself remaining an attractive venue but not one whose institutional capacity has grown in their direction during this period.

## Audience lens — institution

RMBL through this horizon becomes the institution that protected a centennial-scale ecological record when protection was not guaranteed, and that built the in-house capacity to extract a century of accumulated meaning. Its identity sharpens around stewardship — of records, of protocols, of the conditions under which independent inquiry continues — rather than around expansion. The technical core grows materially but remains small in absolute terms, true to the catalytic-not-directive operating model. The Knowledge Commons matures into a substantive discovery and synthesis platform that is recognizably an institutional product, not just an external grant deliverable. Institutional independence, named as a strategic asset in 2026, is operationally protected by reserves and diversified funding that the campaign builds. The cost is that RMBL does not visibly expand into watershed integration, regional water translation, or community co-production at the rate a different scenario would have produced. The institution arrives at its centennial as steward of something rare, with the foundation from which a broader next-horizon plan can responsibly engage the frontiers this campaign chose not to pursue.

## Overlay robustness

**Central case.** Under gradual federal contraction, gradual AI evolution, and an RCP6.0 climate trajectory, the scenario plays out roughly as described. Federal contraction makes the stewardship rationale more compelling rather than less; the long records remain among the few sources able to resolve the questions a changing climate poses. Gradual AI evolution is well-matched to the scenario's pace of in-house capacity growth — by mid-horizon, methods are mature enough for substantive synthesis, but not so transformative that the in-house investment is leapfrogged by external capacity. RCP6.0 climate continues the warming and snowmelt trends the long records have been documenting, sharpening the scientific value of those records year by year. The campaign closes near its target with the long records protected and the in-house synthesis capacity at working scale.

**Stress case — Sharper federal contraction with partial recovery late.** Under sharper federal contraction in the late 2020s and early 2030s, the stewardship investments prove their value early: reserves absorb funding gaps that would otherwise have ended long-record programs, and the in-house capacity that the campaign builds is what carries several guest-scientist projects through their host-institution disruptions. Partial federal recovery in the mid-to-late 2030s finds RMBL with an unusual asset — protected long records and mature in-house synthesis capacity — that recovers external funding draws back more rapidly than at peer institutions. The forgone frontiers remain forgone, but the stewardship choice looks more clearly correct in retrospect.

**Stress case — Faster AI evolution outpaces in-house capacity.** If AI methods evolve faster than the scenario's modest in-house growth can match, the synthesis frontier risks being captured by external research groups with much larger AI capacity, and RMBL's in-house investment risks looking undersized. The scenario adapts by leaning more heavily on methodological partnerships with external groups while concentrating in-house capacity on the data-curation, archival-integration, and basin-specific knowledge layers that external groups cannot easily replicate. The stewardship-of-records core remains intact and arguably more valuable, since the records become the scarce input that external AI capacity needs.

**Stress case — Climate trajectory closer to RCP8.5.** Under a higher-warming trajectory, basin systems cross thresholds the long records were not designed to span, and questions arise about whether continued investment in protocols designed for the prior climate regime remains the right priority. The scenario adapts by treating the long records as the baseline against which the transformed systems must be compared — making them more valuable, not less — while accepting that some active experimental and observational programs that the forgone integration frontiers would have built are visibly missing. The pressure to broaden in the next campaign becomes stronger.

## Plausibility caveats

The scenario assumes that the federal-funding environment continues to make stewardship of long records institutionally vulnerable enough to justify a continuity-heavy campaign, but not so catastrophic that a campaign of this magnitude cannot be raised. It assumes that the guest-scientist communities carrying the long-record programs remain willing and able to continue under formalized succession arrangements, and that institutional transitions in those communities can be managed without losing tacit knowledge. It assumes that AI methods evolve at a pace where modest in-house growth produces load-bearing capacity rather than being immediately outpaced. It assumes donor appetite for a stewardship-framed campaign — which is a less visible proposition than expansion-framed alternatives and which depends on donor recognition of long records as a rare asset. The structural blind spots include: this scenario treats the long records as bounded and protectable, when in fact ecological transformation under continued warming may make some records less interpretable against their own histories; it underweights the possibility that the forgone frontiers (water translation, community co-production, atmospheric integration) become institutionally urgent in ways that strain the stewardship posture mid-campaign; and it assumes that institutional independence is protected primarily through reserves and diversified funding, when in fact independence may be tested in ways those instruments do not address.

## Looking further out: 2040–2050

*Speculative. Lower resolution than the primary horizon.*

Looking forward speculatively from 2040 toward 2050: if the stewardship campaign closes as described, the early 2040s find RMBL in a position to engage the forgone frontiers from a secured foundation. A next-horizon campaign — likely organized around watershed integration, regional water translation, and community co-production — can be undertaken without the central anxiety about long-record continuity that this scenario was built to address. The marmot record passes its centennial in the early 2030s and the meadow phenology series approaches its in the 2040s, both as institutionally protected programs rather than fragile inheritances. The in-house data-science and AI-methods capacity built during the stewardship campaign continues to mature and is increasingly the layer through which guest-scientist work reaches external audiences. By 2050, if these things hold, RMBL is recognizable as an institution whose distinctive contribution is the centennial-scale record stewarded into a period when such records are extraordinarily rare globally, and whose catalytic infrastructure has matured into a synthesis platform that extends well beyond the basin's borders. All of this is contingent on choices not yet made and on conditions — climate, funding, institutional — that the scenario cannot specify. The coda is offered as one plausible extension, not as a forecast.
