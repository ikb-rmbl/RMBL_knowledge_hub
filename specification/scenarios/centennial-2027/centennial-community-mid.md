# Centennial Community-Anchored

*A Future Scenario in the `centennial-2027` set. Mid-range magnitude, balanced. v1.0 under [Future Scenarios Framework v0.7](../../Future_scenarios_framework.md).*

---

## Synopsis

Centennial Community-Anchored assumes the campaign comes in mid-range, and that RMBL makes community partnership a defining feature of how it does basin science. The basin's longest records — the marmot study now in its eighth decade, the meadow phenology series, the snowmelt-driven plant work — continue without lapse. A small data team grows, enough to begin reading a century of records at scales prior generations could not attempt. But the strategic anchor is RMBL365, the year-round building in Crested Butte, renovated and staffed to host ongoing partnerships with the Ute Indian Tribe, Gunnison Valley water districts, and county schools. About half the work is continuity stewardship; the other half builds community partnership capacity and modest data tools. The scenario forgoes new atmospheric instrumentation, large mechanistic experiments, and substantial financial reserves. It asks donors and the institution to test whether RMBL's longstanding commitment to its surrounding community can become a campaign-defining feature rather than a quiet background commitment.

### Campaign deliverables

- **primary.** Renovate RMBL365 into a year-round community-facing hub: laboratory and workshop space, meeting rooms designed for tribal and water-district partners, classroom space for county schools, and a small permanent staff presence in Crested Butte through the off-season.
- **primary.** Build a small partnership-coordination team — a few staff at scales appropriate to a small nonprofit — whose work is to sustain ongoing relationships with the Ute Indian Tribe, Gunnison Valley water districts, county schools, and Forest Service partners across years, not project cycles.
- **secondary.** Protect the marmot, meadow phenology, and snowmelt records through the period: protocol stewardship, plot infrastructure, archival systems, and the technical support that lets guest scientists carry the records forward.
- **secondary.** Grow RMBL's data scientists and archivists modestly — enough capacity to begin digitizing a century of field notebooks and to support guest scientists using AI tools on basin records.
- **supporting.** Develop co-produced research streams where community partners help shape questions: water-quality monitoring with downstream districts, phenology and pollinator work with schools and tribal natural-resources offices, snowpack records read alongside ranching and water-management calendars.
- **continuity_residual.** Modest growth in the savings and broader funding base that buffer RMBL against federal funding shocks, sufficient to keep the partnership work stable across grant cycles without aiming at full independence buffering.

### Frontier support strategies

- **F.cont.1.** The campaign protects the basin's longest continuous records — marmots, meadows, snowmelt — through targeted investment in protocol stewardship, plot infrastructure, archival systems, and the small technical staff that supports guest scientists carrying the records forward. The records' continuity is treated as a non-negotiable floor underneath everything else the scenario does.
- **F.innov.3.** The campaign funds modest growth in RMBL's data scientists and archivists, and the digitization of a century of field notebooks and plot records. The aim is not a flagship AI program but a capacity sized to support guest scientists who want to read basin records at new scales, and to make those records legible to community partners as well as academic ones.
- **F.innov.4.** The campaign anchors community-science partnerships in the renovated RMBL365 building, which becomes a year-round venue for ongoing work with the Ute Indian Tribe, Gunnison Valley water districts, and county schools. A small partnership-coordination staff sustains these relationships across years; co-produced research streams give partners a real role in shaping basin questions, not just receiving findings.

### Forgone

This scenario does not fund new atmospheric or watershed instrumentation. The SAIL-style integration of clouds, snow, soil, and biota that defined the early 2020s continues only insofar as guest scientists bring outside grants for it. The campaign does not build large mechanistic experimental platforms — no new common gardens, no factorial warming arrays, no basin-scale paired-treatment networks. It does not fund a flagship AI synthesis program; the data work stays modest and service-oriented. It does not build substantial financial reserves; the savings layer is enough to keep partnership work stable across grant cycles but not enough to fully buffer RMBL against deep federal contraction. Cross-mountain collaboration with peer field stations grows only as community-partnership work pulls it in. The campaign chooses community depth over scientific breadth, and accepts that several promising directions stay on hold or move slowly through this period.

### Seeds in the present

The seeds of this scenario are already visible. RMBL purchased the Crested Butte hostel in 2025 and rebranded it RMBL365 — a year-round, in-town building that the campaign would renovate and staff. The 2021–25 era saw rising press attention to basin science, especially around the gap between snowpack and runoff and around insect decline; downstream water managers and tribal natural-resources offices are already among the audiences for that work. The planning themes identify wildlife-recreation interactions, water governance, mountain-community land-use policy, and land-management translation as areas where stakeholders are actively waiting on evidence. RMBL's small technical core — the geospatial data scientist, GIS manager, and instrumentation technicians — provides the data backbone the scenario builds modestly on. The marmot study, the meadow phenology record, and the snowmelt-driven plant work are mature enough that protocol stewardship has become a defined institutional task, not a background assumption.

## Setting

This scenario takes as its starting assumption that RMBL's longstanding commitment to its surrounding community can become a campaign-defining feature, not just an institutional background value. The campaign comes in at the middle of what RMBL might raise — substantial but not at the upper end — and the strategic question is what such a campaign should anchor on.

The answer this scenario tests is RMBL365. The building RMBL bought in Crested Butte in 2025 is renovated through the late 2020s into a year-round venue with laboratory space, meeting rooms designed for tribal and water-district partners, and classroom space for county schools. A small partnership-coordination team is hired and based there through the off-season. Their job is not to run projects but to sustain relationships with the Ute Indian Tribe, the Upper Gunnison River Water Conservancy District, the Colorado River Water Conservation District, the Forest Service, and the school district across years.

Underneath that anchor, the basin's longest records continue. The marmot demographic study now in its eighth decade. The meadow phenology series and the snowmelt-driven plant work are protected through targeted stewardship investments. A small data team grows, enough to begin digitizing a century of field notebooks and to support guest scientists using AI tools on basin records.

What the scenario does not do is build new atmospheric instrumentation, large mechanistic experiments, or substantial financial reserves. Federal funding stays tight through the period, and the partnership work has to prove itself as a stable funding stream — through foundations, private donors, and partner contributions — rather than relying on federal grants. The campaign treats community partnership as catalytic infrastructure: durable, place-based, and capable of compounding.

## The arc — three phases across the primary horizon

### Phase 1: Renovation and relationship-building (2026–2030)

The first phase is RMBL365's transformation. By 2028 the building has been renovated: laboratory space for guest scientists staging field campaigns, a meeting room designed for ongoing work with tribal and water-district partners, classroom space the school district can use through the school year. A small partnership-coordination team is hired by 2029. Their first eighteen months are spent listening — meeting with the Ute Indian Tribe's natural-resources office, with the Upper Gunnison River Water Conservancy District, with Forest Service planners, with the school district, with county commissioners. The marmot study passes its seventy-fifth year in 2038, and a modest seventy-fifth-year event at RMBL365 marks the milestone for both scientific and community audiences. The data team grows by one or two positions: a digital archivist begins working through a century of field notebooks, and an additional data scientist supports guest researchers. The campaign closes near the middle of the realistic range. Federal contraction continues through the phase, and the campaign's mid-range scale already reflects that the partnership work has to be funded from foundations, individuals, and partner contributions rather than federal grants.

### Phase 2: Co-produced research takes shape (2031–2035)

By the early 2030s, the partnership work starts producing visible co-produced research. The Ute Indian Tribe's natural-resources office and RMBL run a multi-year project on culturally important plants and their phenology in the basin, with the tribe's staff shaping the questions. The Upper Gunnison and Colorado River water districts and RMBL maintain a paired record of snowpack, soil moisture, and streamflow read alongside ranching and water-management calendars; the records are presented annually at district meetings. County schools run a phenology and pollinator monitoring program in partnership with RMBL, and high-school students contribute observations that enter the long record. The data team finishes digitizing the marmot field notebooks and most of the meadow phenology archive. Guest scientists begin publishing AI-assisted reanalyses of the long records — modest in scale but real. The marmot, meadow, and snowmelt records continue without lapse. Federal funding remains tight but somewhat stable; the campaign's diversified base holds the partnership work steady across the phase.

### Phase 3: Partnership as durable feature (2036–2040)

By the late 2030s, RMBL's partnership with its surrounding community has become a durable feature of how the institution does its work, not a project the campaign funded. The partnership-coordination team is stable. The Ute Indian Tribe, Gunnison Valley water districts, the Forest Service, and the school district are not audiences for basin findings but participants in shaping what is studied. The co-produced research streams have produced enough evidence to begin informing BLM and Forest Service planning revisions, water-court testimony, and county land-use decisions, on the timescales those processes actually use. The marmot study, now in its eighth decade, continues. The meadow phenology and snowmelt records continue without interruption. The data team has begun working with guest scientists on second-generation AI analyses that pull together a century of records across taxa. What the period has built is a model — visible to peer field stations and to funders — of a basin science that is genuinely co-produced with the community in which it happens. What it has not built is large new instrumentation, deep financial reserves, or independence from federal funding cycles. Those choices come due in the next campaign.

## Lines of inquiry

Several research threads mature through the period. The marmot study, now in its eighth decade, becomes the centerpiece of co-produced work with the Ute Indian Tribe and the school district: a long-record animal that schoolchildren and tribal staff help observe and that AI analysis begins reading at new scales. The meadow phenology and snowmelt-driven plant work continues, and the modest data capacity lets guest scientists pull a century of bloom and snowmelt records into modern syntheses. Co-produced research with water districts matures into a multi-decade paired record of snowpack, soil moisture, runoff timing, and downstream delivery — read alongside ranching and water-management calendars, and increasingly cited in water-court and Colorado Water Conservation Board proceedings. Phenology and pollinator monitoring runs through the schools as a sustained program. Work shaped by the tribe on culturally important plants and on traditional knowledge of seasonal change becomes a durable thread. What does not mature in this scenario: SAIL-scale atmospheric integration, large factorial experiments, deep mechanistic work on plant-microbe-soil coupling, and broad cross-mountain comparative ecology. Those threads continue only insofar as guest scientists bring outside grants for them. The basin's identity through this period is as a place where ecology is done with the community it studies, not as a place where every promising frontier advances.

## Moments of choice within the primary horizon

1. **2028 — How to split the campaign between endowment and capital projects.** *Shared inflection: `endowment-capital-split-2028`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and board
   - Alternatives: Heavier endowment to protect long-term operations; heavier capital to renovate RMBL365 and build out infrastructure quickly.
   - Scenario assumption: The split leans toward capital in the near term — RMBL365 renovation needs front-loaded investment — with endowment growth weighted toward partnership-coordination staffing rather than financial reserves.

2. **2029 — Whether to anchor the campaign's external identity around community partnership or around scientific stewardship.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the Development & Advancement team
   - Alternatives: Lead with the seventy-fifth-year marmot story and records protection; lead with RMBL365 and the partnership model; present them as equal pillars.
   - Scenario assumption: The campaign leads with community partnership as the defining feature, with records stewardship described as the foundation underneath it. This is a real bet about which framing the donor base responds to.

3. **2030 — What governance structure to put in place for co-produced research.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, the Ute Indian Tribe's natural-resources office, and water-district partners
   - Alternatives: Project-by-project agreements as they arise; a standing advisory body that helps shape RMBL's research priorities; formal partnership agreements with shared decision-making over specific research streams.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL adopts formal partnership agreements for specific research streams (the culturally important plants work, the water-district paired record), and a standing advisory body for broader basin priorities. This is more demanding than a project-by-project posture and reshapes how RMBL makes some of its scientific decisions.

4. **2032 — Which long records receive priority stewardship investment as funding stays constrained.** *Shared inflection: `stewardship-priority-2032`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and senior science staff
   - Alternatives: Spread stewardship thinly across all long records; concentrate on the marmot record now in its eighth decade; concentrate on phenology and snowmelt; protect a defined set and accept gaps elsewhere.
   - Scenario assumption: The campaign protects the marmot, meadow phenology, and snowmelt-driven plant work as a defined set, and accepts that some shorter-running observation programs may go on hiatus during the period.

5. **2034 — Whether to deepen the partnership work into formal land-management translation — testimony in water court, Forest Service plan revisions, BLM Resource Management Plan input — or keep it at the relationship and co-produced-research level.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the partnership-coordination team
   - Alternatives: Stay at the relationship and research level; build modest translation capacity that prepares basin records for specific agency proceedings; build a substantial translation function with dedicated staff.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL adds modest translation capacity — a portion of one or two staff members' time — to prepare basin records for specific agency and water-court proceedings when partners request it. A larger translation function is left for a future campaign.

6. **2038 — How to position the institution as the campaign closes and the next planning cycle begins.** *Shared inflection: `next-horizon-2040`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the board
   - Alternatives: Consolidate around the partnership model and seek to deepen it; pivot the next campaign toward the financial reserves and atmospheric infrastructure this scenario forgoes; pursue both in sequence.
   - Scenario assumption: The institution treats partnership as a durable feature now woven into how it operates, and points the next planning cycle at the financial reserves and instrumentation gaps this period left untouched.

## Audience lens — research

For a scientist considering bringing research to the basin during this period, the question is whether co-produced work appeals to you. RMBL365 is a real venue: laboratory space, meeting rooms, and a partnership team that can connect you to the Ute Indian Tribe's natural-resources office, water districts, the Forest Service, and the schools. If your work touches phenology, pollinators, snowpack, water quality, or culturally important plants, those connections will shape what you can ask and how your findings travel. The long records continue, and modest data capacity helps guest scientists read them at new scales. What you will not find here is new atmospheric instrumentation at the scale SAIL built, large factorial experiments, or a flagship AI synthesis program. Those who want the deepest mechanistic work or the broadest cross-mountain comparisons may find their fits elsewhere. Those who want their basin science to be done with the people who live in the basin will find this period a strong moment to come.

## Audience lens — institution

For someone on staff or the board, RMBL during this period feels like an institution that has become more deeply local. The Crested Butte building is in use through the winter — meetings with water-district partners in February, a tribal advisory session in March, a school-group visit in October. The partnership-coordination team is small but present year-round, and what they do reshapes some of what gets studied in Gothic each summer. The marmot study seventy-fifth year in 2026 is a community event, not just a scientific one. The data team grows enough that the long records become more readable, but the institutional growth is modest. The instrumentation core stays close to its 2026 footprint. There are real tradeoffs felt internally: the lack of deep financial reserves means each funding shock matters, and some promising scientific directions move slowly or not at all. What it gains is a kind of community grounding that is harder to lose than equipment is.

## Audience lens — donor

A gift to this campaign joins an effort to make community partnership a defining feature of how basin science gets done. Your contribution is part of renovating RMBL365 into a year-round venue where the Ute Indian Tribe, water districts, schools, and Forest Service partners are not audiences for findings but participants in shaping what is studied. It supports the small team that sustains those relationships across years. It enables the conditions under which the basin's longest records — the marmot study in its eighth decade, the meadow phenology series, the snowmelt-driven plant work — continue without lapse, and under which a modest data team begins reading a century of records at new scales. This campaign is not the largest RMBL could mount, and it leaves some scientific directions for a future cycle. What it offers is a bet that basin science done with the surrounding community compounds in ways that matter beyond what any single project can produce.

## Overlay robustness

**Central case.** Under gradual federal contraction, a moderate evolution of AI tools, and a mid-range climate path, the scenario plays out close to its central description. RMBL365 is renovated and staffed by the late 2020s. The partnership team builds durable relationships through the early 2030s, and co-produced research streams mature. The long records continue without lapse, and modest data capacity is enough to support guest scientists using new tools. The campaign's diversified base, while not deep, is enough to keep the partnership work stable across the period. By 2040 community partnership is a real feature of how RMBL operates, not just a campaign deliverable.

**Stress case — Deeper federal contraction and a foundation pullback.** If federal funding contracts more sharply and one or two foundations central to the partnership work step back, the modest savings layer is not enough to hold the partnership team together through a multi-year gap. RMBL likely keeps the records stewardship intact and reduces the partnership team to one or two positions through the contraction. The co-produced research streams slow but do not collapse. The Crested Butte building stays open year-round but at lower programming intensity. By 2040 the partnership model is intact but less developed than the central case projects. The lesson the institution carries forward is that mid-range campaigns without deep reserves are exposed in ways the next planning cycle has to address.

**Stress case — Faster AI evolution and a basin-records AI breakthrough.** If AI tools mature faster than the central case assumes, and guest scientists begin producing major retrospective syntheses of basin records by the early 2030s, the demand for data capacity outruns what this scenario funded. RMBL likely keeps the partnership commitments intact and finds bridge funding for additional data scientists, but the work strains the institution. The partnership team helps translate the new findings to the community and to land managers, which becomes one of the scenario's most visible outputs. By 2040 the basin's research identity is shaped both by community partnership and by AI-enabled record analysis at a scale the scenario did not plan for. The next campaign starts with a clearer sense of how much data capacity the period actually demanded.

**Stress case — Sharper climate shifts in the basin.** If snowpack thinning accelerates and ecosystems cross thresholds faster than the central case assumes, the long records become more scientifically urgent and the water-district partnership becomes more institutionally urgent. The partnership-coordination team finds itself in more frequent demand from agency partners and water managers. RMBL likely accelerates the modest translation capacity it built in the mid-2030s. The lack of new atmospheric instrumentation is felt — basin scientists are watching faster change with the instruments the 2020s left behind — but the community-anchored work matters more, not less, as decisions about water and land become more consequential. By 2040 the partnership model looks well-timed for the moment the basin is in.

## Plausibility caveats

This scenario assumes that the donor base will respond to community partnership as a campaign-defining feature, not just as a supporting commitment. That is a real bet. RMBL's traditional donor base has been shaped by the scientific work, and whether donors will give at mid-range magnitude to a campaign whose external identity leads with RMBL365 and partnership relationships is genuinely uncertain. If the answer is no, the campaign either falls short of its target or quietly shifts its emphasis toward records stewardship — which would leave the partnership work underfunded relative to what this scenario describes.

The scenario also assumes that the Ute Indian Tribe, water districts, and school district will engage at the depth the partnership model requires. Co-produced research is more demanding than consultative outreach; it asks partners for sustained time and decision-making attention. If institutional capacity on partner sides is thinner than this scenario assumes, the work happens but at lower intensity than the central case describes.

A characteristic failure mode for this scenario is that the partnership work absorbs more institutional attention than it generates external funding for. The partnership team is small, and if foundations and individual donors do not sustain the partnership work as a fundable program over fifteen years, the scientific work and the partnership work compete for the same RMBL attention in ways that erode both.

A structural blind spot: the scenario does not account well for what happens if federal contraction is sharp enough to threaten the long records themselves. The modest savings layer is sized for the partnership work, not for protecting the records through a deep funding crisis. The records would survive a moderate shock; a severe one would force tradeoffs this scenario has not modeled.

Finally, the scenario assumes that community partnership translates into research and land-management outcomes within fifteen years. Some translation timescales — water-court proceedings, Forest Service plan revisions — operate on cycles long enough that the campaign period may end before the partnership work produces its most visible results.

## Looking further out: 2040–2050

*Speculative. Lower resolution than the primary horizon.*

Looking past 2040 toward 2050, this scenario's lasting consequence is the institutional shape RMBL has settled into. The basin is studied with the surrounding community rather than from a distance. RMBL's place in the region — as scientific home, as community resource, as a working partner of the Ute Indian Tribe and the water districts — has deepened from periodic visibility into ongoing presence. The 2040s likely see a successor campaign that addresses what this one left undone: deeper financial reserves, atmospheric instrumentation, perhaps a more substantial translation function. The partnership model built in this period becomes the platform from which that next campaign launches, not a deliverable that needs defending. The long records continue with the marmot study approaching its ninetieth year. By 2050 the question of whether community partnership can be a defining feature of a basin field station has been answered — not in the abstract but through the durable relationships and co-produced records this period built. The further future remains genuinely uncertain, and this coda is offered as one plausible trajectory rather than a forecast.
