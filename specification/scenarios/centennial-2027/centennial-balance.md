# Centennial Balance

*A Future Scenario in the `centennial-2027` set. Mid-range magnitude, balanced. v1.0 under [Future Scenarios Framework v0.7](../../Future_scenarios_framework.md).*

---

## Synopsis

Centennial Balance assumes the campaign comes in at mid-range, and that RMBL responds by doing several things moderately well rather than betting everything on one. The marmot study reaches its hundredth year during this period. The long meadow phenology series and the snowmelt-driven plant work continue without lapse. Alongside that stewardship, RMBL grows a small data team that can read a century of records at scales prior generations could not attempt, and builds the savings and broader funding base that protect its independence through funding shocks. Roughly fifty-five percent of the work is continuity; the rest is data capacity and institutional resilience. The scenario forgoes new atmospheric instrumentation, broad community co-production work, and large mechanistic experimental platforms. It tests something specific about donors: whether a three-part bet — records, data, independence — is more compelling than a single anchor. It asks the institution to hold a balanced portfolio together without letting any one part starve the others.

### Campaign deliverables

- **primary.** Stewardship of the basin's longest continuous records — the marmot study, the long meadow phenology series, and the snowmelt-driven plant work. This covers the protocols, plot infrastructure, observer-succession arrangements with guest scientists, and archival systems that keep these records intact through the centennial period. This is the largest single area of investment.
- **primary.** Growth of RMBL's small data team — adding data scientists, a research-archivist role, and AI-methods capacity to the existing geospatial and GIS staff. This team digitizes a century of field notebooks and plot records, builds the tools that make them machine-readable, and partners with guest scientists doing retrospective analysis.
- **secondary.** Savings and governance work that buffer RMBL against funding shocks and protect institutional independence — a meaningful operating reserve, the development staffing to broaden the funding base beyond federal sources, and the governance infrastructure that keeps inquiry unrestricted. Sized to make a real difference without dominating the campaign.
- **supporting.** Modest renovation at RMBL365 in Crested Butte to support year-round staff working on archival digitization and data-team functions that benefit from in-town, year-round space rather than seasonal Gothic facilities. This is a distinctive deliverable for this scenario: the building serves the data and stewardship work, not a broad community-engagement program.
- **continuity_residual.** Maintenance of the basic technical infrastructure — instrumentation upkeep, cyberinfrastructure, the Knowledge Commons platform — that the broader guest-scientist community depends on regardless of which frontiers the campaign emphasizes.

### Frontier support strategies

- **F.cont.1.** Direct stewardship investment: protocol documentation, plot and observer succession, archival systems, and the technical staff that supports guest scientists carrying the long records forward through the centennial period. This is the largest commitment in the scenario and the foundation on which the other two frontiers depend.
- **F.innov.3.** Growth of RMBL's in-house data team — a few new positions in data science, AI methods, and archival work — paired with digitization of a century of basin records. The team builds tools and partners with guest scientists rather than pursuing its own research agenda. This frontier depends on the records work for source material.
- **F.innov.5.** Building the operating reserves, broadened funding base, and governance capacity that keep RMBL independent through funding shocks. Less visible than the other two frontiers but equally deliberate: the conditions under which the records and the data work can continue regardless of what happens to federal science budgets.

### Forgone

This scenario does not fund new atmospheric or watershed instrumentation. The integration work that SAIL began in the East River continues through guest scientists and partner institutions, but RMBL does not build the next generation of that infrastructure during this period. The scenario also does not launch a broad community co-production program — the partnerships with tribal nations, water districts, and stakeholder organizations that a community-facing scenario would build are not the work of this campaign. Large mechanistic experimental platforms — the factorial warming and drought experiments that would let guest scientists test phenology mismatch at depth — are not funded. New cross-mountain partnership infrastructure for hosting peer institutions is also not pursued. Each of these is a real frontier the basin needs; this scenario chooses to let other sources, other periods, or other campaigns address them, and to use the mid-range campaign for records, data capacity, and independence instead.

### Seeds in the present

The scenario builds on RMBL's existing technical core — the geospatial data scientist, GIS manager, and instrumentation technicians who already maintain the catalytic infrastructure on which guest-scientist research depends. The Knowledge Commons itself is one product of the recent pattern where outside grants build RMBL's own tools, which then attract more grants. The 2025 acquisition of RMBL365 in Crested Butte is the existing year-round, in-town platform the scenario uses for data-team work. The long records this scenario protects are well-documented in the basin's recent and longer-arc primers: the marmot demographic record now approaching its centennial, the long meadow phenology series, and the snowmelt-driven plant demography work. The planning themes that converge most directly on this scenario's bets are the long-term integrated demographic and genetic time series theme, the integrated monitoring and remote-sensing infrastructure theme, and the mechanistic field ecology theme — though this scenario serves the first two more than the third.

## Setting

This scenario takes as its starting point a mid-range campaign and a working assumption that RMBL does not have to choose between protecting its longest records, building the capacity to read them in new ways, and shoring up the financial independence that keeps both possible. The campaign is substantial but not at the upper end of what RMBL might raise. The bet is that a balanced three-part portfolio is more strategically coherent than a single anchor, and that donors will respond to a campaign that promises to do several important things moderately well.

Federal science funding stays tight throughout the period. The contraction that has already affected RMBL-associated researchers does not lift; it stabilizes as the new baseline. RMBL's independence as a nonprofit field station becomes more visible as a feature, not just an organizational fact. The basin's longest records — the marmot study, the meadow phenology series, the snowmelt-driven plant work — are entering decades where any lapse would be irreparable, and the centennial of the marmot work arrives within the horizon. AI tools mature steadily enough that a century of accumulated field notebooks, plot data, and observer records becomes newly readable at scales prior generations could not attempt.

The scenario's balance is deliberate. About fifty-five percent of the work is continuity stewardship. The rest is roughly split between growing RMBL's small data team and building the savings and broader funding base that protect independence. None of these three commitments is large enough on its own to dominate the campaign's identity. The discipline the scenario asks of RMBL is to hold the balance — to let each commitment do its work without letting any one starve the others.

## The arc — three phases across the primary horizon

### Phase 1: Setting the balance (2026–2030)

The campaign launches and reaches its mid-range target by 2028 or 2029. RMBL's leadership and board negotiate the split between immediate stewardship needs and the longer-horizon savings that protect independence. The first new data-team hires arrive — a data scientist and a research archivist working with the existing geospatial staff. They begin the multi-year work of digitizing field notebooks and plot records from across the basin's long studies, starting with the marmot records and the meadow phenology series. Stewardship arrangements for the long records are formalized with the guest scientists who carry them, including succession planning that the next phase will test. Modest renovation at RMBL365 in Crested Butte creates year-round workspace for the data and archival work. The operating reserves grow more slowly than some on the board want, because the immediate stewardship needs are real and arrive first. By 2030 the balanced portfolio is visible: records are intact, the data team is small but real, and the reserves are growing.

### Phase 2: The centennial and the digital archive (2031–2035)

The marmot study reaches its hundredth year. The centennial is marked quietly — a symposium, a public-facing piece in the Knowledge Commons, partnership with the guest scientists who have carried the work — and the records themselves continue without lapse. The digitization work matures: by 2034 a substantial fraction of the basin's archival records are machine-readable, and the first wave of retrospective syntheses using AI tools appears in the literature, led by guest scientists working with the RMBL data team. The data team itself has grown to four or five people. The savings reach a level that meaningfully buffers RMBL against a federal funding shock. A test of this comes in the early 2030s when a competing institution loses a major federal program; RMBL's reserves and broader funding base let it absorb a partial transfer of basin-relevant work without scrambling. The balanced portfolio holds. The scenario's central tension — that no one commitment dominates — also begins to show: some donors and some researchers want RMBL to lean harder into one of the three. The leadership holds the balance.

### Phase 3: Compounding quietly (2036–2040)

The investments compound. The long records, intact and increasingly machine-readable, become the empirical backbone for a generation of climate-attribution work the basin could not have supported in 2026. The data team is now central to how guest scientists work in the basin; it is not large, but it is mature, and its tools and methods are widely used. The savings and broader funding base have changed RMBL's relationship to federal funding shocks: contractions still hurt, but they no longer threaten institutional continuity. RMBL365 has become the working home of the year-round data and archival work. By 2040 none of the three commitments has dominated; each has done its quiet work. The institutional question that arrives at the end of the horizon is what the next campaign should look like — whether the balanced posture should continue, or whether the conditions have shifted enough to warrant a sharper bet on one of the frontiers the scenario chose to let mature.

## Lines of inquiry

The long phenology, demography, and pollinator records mature through the horizon and remain the basin's empirical backbone. The marmot work continues into its second century with social-network, demographic, and molecular threads woven together. The meadow phenology series and the snowmelt-driven plant work continue without lapse, feeding into climate-attribution research at scales only long records can support. Retrospective AI-assisted synthesis of the digitized archives becomes a real research line by the middle of the horizon, led by guest scientists working with RMBL's data team. Phenology mismatch questions deepen as the digitized records expose patterns that were not visible in the published literature. The atmospheric and watershed integration work that SAIL began continues through guest scientists and partner institutions, but is not the campaign's focus; new instrumentation in that area comes from external grants rather than campaign capital. Broad community co-production work and large mechanistic experimental platforms remain the work of other periods or other funders. What matures is what the campaign chose: records, the capacity to read them, and the institutional resilience that protects both.

## Moments of choice within the primary horizon

1. **2028 — How to split campaign proceeds between immediate capital and stewardship needs and the longer-horizon savings that protect independence.** *Shared inflection: `endowment-capital-split-2028`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and Development & Advancement team
   - Alternatives: A heavier tilt toward immediate stewardship and data-team capacity (more visible early results, less buffer); a heavier tilt toward savings (less visible work, stronger buffer); the balanced split this scenario assumes.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL holds a deliberate balance, accepting that the savings grow more slowly than some on the board want.

2. **2029 — Whether the growing data team takes on its own research agenda or stays focused on building tools and supporting guest scientists.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and technical staff
   - Alternatives: Let the data team develop independent research projects as their expertise matures; keep them in a catalytic posture supporting guest-scientist work; a hybrid where a small share of their time is independent.
   - Scenario assumption: The data team stays in a catalytic posture. RMBL does not become a research institution with in-house PIs pursuing independent agendas.

3. **2032 — Which of the basin's long records receive the most concentrated stewardship attention when staffing and observer-succession constraints force a prioritization.** *Shared inflection: `stewardship-priority-2032`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, guest scientists, and board
   - Alternatives: Concentrate on the marmot centennial; concentrate on the meadow phenology series; concentrate on the snowmelt-driven plant work; spread support evenly and accept slower progress on each.
   - Scenario assumption: The marmot centennial gets concentrated attention through 2035, and the meadow and snowmelt work continue with steady but less concentrated support.

4. **2033 — How to respond when a partner institution loses a major federal program and basin-relevant work needs a new home.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and board
   - Alternatives: Decline to absorb the work and preserve focus on the campaign's three commitments; absorb a partial transfer and use the savings to support the transition; expand the data team or other capacity beyond the campaign plan.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL absorbs a partial transfer, using its reserves and broader funding base to support the transition without expanding beyond the campaign plan.

5. **2036 — Whether to launch a mid-horizon campaign extension to fund the atmospheric or community frontiers the original campaign forwent.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, Development & Advancement team, and donors
   - Alternatives: Extend the campaign and add a new frontier; keep the original three commitments and let other frontiers wait; pursue external grants specifically for the forgone frontiers without a campaign extension.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL holds the original three commitments and pursues external grants where guest-scientist demand warrants.

6. **2040 — What the shape of the next campaign should be — whether to continue the balanced posture or sharpen into one of the frontiers this scenario let mature.** *Shared inflection: `next-horizon-2040`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, board, and the broader research community
   - Alternatives: Continue a balanced multi-frontier posture; lean into atmospheric and watershed integration; lean into community and translation work; lean into cross-mountain partnerships.
   - Scenario assumption: The decision is left open for the next period; this scenario does not presume what the next campaign should be.

## Overlay robustness

**Central case.** Federal funding stays tight but does not collapse further. AI tools mature steadily, becoming genuinely useful for archival synthesis by the early 2030s. The climate continues warming along a moderate trajectory; snowpack thins and the basin's records become more valuable as climate-attribution baselines. The balanced portfolio holds. The records continue, the data team grows, the savings compound. By 2040 RMBL is the field station it was, with stronger underlying capacity in records, data, and independence.

**Stress case — Sharper federal contraction.** Federal funding contracts further in the late 2020s. Guest-scientist work in the basin thins. RMBL's savings, still growing, are not yet at the level the scenario planned for. The leadership tilts campaign proceeds harder toward reserves and broader funding-base work in the early 2030s, and the data team grows more slowly than planned. The records are still protected, because that is the first commitment. By 2040 the data and savings commitments have both partially delivered; the balanced portfolio held but with less data capacity than the central case.

**Stress case — Slower-than-expected AI maturation.** AI tools mature more slowly or more unevenly than the scenario expected. The data team's tools work but do not transform retrospective synthesis at the scale anticipated. The digitization work still pays off — machine-readable archives matter regardless of AI — but the dramatic late-horizon syntheses do not arrive. The records and independence commitments deliver as planned. By 2040 the data investment looks more like infrastructure for a future period than a frontier that fully matured.

**Stress case — Sharper climate disruption.** Snowpack thins faster than the central case expected; one or more basin systems crosses a threshold the long records were not designed to capture. Guest-scientist demand shifts toward new questions the campaign did not fund instrumentation for. The records and data team remain valuable, but the gap between what the basin needs and what the campaign built becomes visible. The 2036 choice about a campaign extension becomes more pressing. RMBL's reserves help it respond, but the balanced posture is tested.

## Plausibility caveats

This scenario assumes a mid-range campaign reaches its target on a timeline that lets the three commitments grow together. It assumes donors respond to a balanced three-part bet — records, data capacity, independence — rather than insisting on a sharper single-frontier campaign thesis. This is the central characteristic risk of the scenario: a balanced portfolio may read as diffuse to donors who want a clear story. A campaign that tries to do three things moderately well may end up doing all three less well than a sharper-focused campaign would do one. The scenario also assumes federal funding stabilizes rather than contracting further. It assumes AI tools mature on a timeline that makes the data investment pay off within the horizon. It assumes RMBL's leadership and board can hold the balance for fifteen years without one commitment crowding the others — a discipline the scenario takes for granted but that real institutions find hard to sustain. The structural blind spot is that the scenario describes the basin largely through its existing research traditions. If basin questions reorganize around something the current frontiers do not capture — a new contaminant, a new ecological threshold, a new political constraint — the balanced portfolio may be less responsive than a campaign built around a single, sharper bet would be. The scenario also gives less attention than it could to community priorities and to the partnership work that other scenarios in this set foreground; readers should weigh that omission against their own sense of what RMBL needs to be.

## Looking further out: 2040–2050

*Speculative. Lower resolution than the primary horizon.*

By 2050 the marmot study is well into its second century, the meadow phenology series has become one of the longest continuous plant records anywhere, and retrospective syntheses of basin records using mature AI tools are routine. The data team that started small in the late 2020s is by then an established part of how the basin's science gets done. The savings and broader funding base, built quietly through the campaign period, have buffered RMBL through at least one further federal contraction and probably more. None of these changes are dramatic when seen one at a time. Taken together they have left RMBL stronger than it was at the start of the campaign period — the records intact, the capacity to read them mature, and the institution more resilient. What the next campaign looks like depends on conditions that are not yet visible. The work this scenario funded made it possible for that next campaign to be designed from a position of strength rather than scarcity.
