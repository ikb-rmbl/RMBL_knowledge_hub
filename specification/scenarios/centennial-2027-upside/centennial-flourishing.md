# Centennial Flourishing

*A Future Scenario in the `centennial-2027-upside` set. Target magnitude, balanced. v1.0 under [Future Scenarios Framework v0.15](../../Future_scenarios_framework.md).*

---

## Synopsis

This scenario describes an upside-tail future for RMBL's Centennial Campaign — one where the fundraising comes in roughly three times what the campaign could realistically expect, AI and data tools keep maturing, and public science funding holds steady. Under those stacked-favorable conditions, RMBL protects every long record the basin is known for and also builds the data team, the integrated sensing network, and the year-round community presence at RMBL365 that the institution could not afford to build at a smaller campaign scale. The bet is that growth along every dimension at once, done carefully, leaves RMBL recognizably itself — a small basin-anchored nonprofit serving guest scientists, not a directive research institution. What's forgone is the simplicity and focus of a smaller campaign: this version asks much more of leadership, the board, and the surrounding community in absorbing growth without losing the basin's character. The invitation to donors is to be part of a deliberate, careful expansion that compounds.

### Campaign deliverables

- **primary.** Grow RMBL's data and geospatial team several-fold beyond its 2026 size, so that the basin's long records can be digitized, linked, and made readable to AI-assisted analysis. This is the team that operates the Knowledge Commons, partners with guest scientists on synthesis, and turns archival material into usable data.
- **primary.** Protect and extend the basin's long records — the marmot demographic study, the meadow phenology series, the snowmelt-driven plant work, the pollinator censuses — through archival systems, plot infrastructure, observer-succession partnerships with the guest scientists who carry the records, and the data systems that make the records available across institutions.
- **primary.** Build the permanent sensing and data infrastructure that couples atmosphere, snowpack, hydrology, soil, and ecology across the East River, extending what SAIL began into something RMBL maintains across decades rather than across a single campaign window.
- **secondary.** Renovate RMBL365 into a year-round venue with laboratory space, staging space for guest scientists arriving outside the summer season, and meeting space that supports partnerships with water managers, Forest Service planners, and tribal natural-resources offices. The building already exists; the campaign deepens what it can do.
- **secondary.** Anchor a cross-mountain partnership network with peer field stations, with RMBL as the convening institution and the basin's long records as the comparative reference. This is partnership-coordination capacity, hosting infrastructure, and a small dedicated staff function — not an endowed program of independent investigators.
- **supporting.** Build savings that buffer RMBL against future funding shocks and protect the institution's independence. This is the unglamorous deliverable that makes the rest of the scenario survivable across the decades the long records require.

### Frontier support strategies

- **F.cont.1.** The campaign treats the basin's longest records as global infrastructure. It funds archival systems, protocol stewardship, observer-succession partnerships with the guest scientists who carry each record, and the data team that links the records to each other and to outside datasets.
- **F.cont.2.** The marmot demographic record reaches its seventy-fifth year in 2038 inside this campaign window. The campaign protects instrumentation continuity at the colonies above Gothic, archival access to the long mark-recapture record, and the infrastructure that supports the guest scientists carrying the work forward.
- **F.cont.3.** Plot infrastructure, protocol stewardship, and data systems for the meadow demography and snowmelt-driven plant work. The campaign also funds the digitization that makes decades of field notes available to retrospective analysis.
- **F.cont.4.** Census continuity infrastructure and network data systems for the pollinator records, integrated with the phenology and floral-trait work so that interaction-network change can be read against the same climate record.
- **F.innov.1.** Permanent integrative sensing across atmosphere, snowpack, hydrology, soil chemistry, and ecological response in the East River. The campaign extends what SAIL and the watershed testbed began into infrastructure RMBL maintains across decades, supported by the geospatial team that links sensor streams to ecological observations.
- **F.innov.3.** The campaign's most distinctive bet. It funds the digitization of a century of basin records, the in-house data scientists who build AI-assisted synthesis tools on top of them, and the methodological partnerships with guest scientists who use the tools. This is where the larger data team is genuinely load-bearing — synthesis at this scale depends on infrastructure RMBL operates, not only on outside researchers passing through.

### Forgone

This scenario does not become a directive research institution. RMBL does not hire a portfolio of independent investigators pursuing their own research agendas; the guest-scientist model remains the core of how basin research happens. The campaign does not fund speculative new facilities in Gothic that compete with maintaining what exists. It does not expand into a generic field-station role serving any visiting researcher; the focus stays on the basin's distinctive long records and the integrated mountain-system work that builds on them. The campaign also forgoes the simplicity of a smaller, more focused campaign — growth along every dimension at once is harder to manage than growth along one. The risk that RMBL stops feeling like RMBL is real, and the campaign accepts the cost of slower hiring, more deliberate partnerships, and more board attention to institutional culture than a smaller campaign would require.

### Seeds in the present

The recent pattern where outside grants build RMBL's own tools and capacity, which then attract more grants, is the foundation. The Knowledge Commons itself is one product of that pattern; the geospatial and GIS team that operates it is another. SAIL brought atmospheric and watershed instrumentation into the East River starting in 2021, and the integrated mountain-system science that followed shows what permanent infrastructure of this kind can support. The marmot study at sixty-four years, the meadow phenology series past its half-century, the pollinator censuses, and the snowmelt-driven plant work are the long records the scenario protects. RMBL365 was purchased in 2025 and is already in service as a year-round venue. The federal funding disruption of the past year has made diversified support an institutional priority. Across all of these, the planning themes point at the same thing: integration infrastructure — shared records, coupled models, durable interfaces with land managers — is the rate-limiting step.

## Setting

This scenario takes as its starting condition that the Centennial Campaign substantially over-performs. The campaign closes well above the realistic ceiling, in the upside tail of what RMBL might plausibly raise. Several favorable conditions stack to make this possible. The basin's centennial framing draws unusually generous response from major donors, foundations, and individual giving. AI and data tools continue to mature without a capability winter. Public science funding stays at least flat in real terms, so the outside grants that have built RMBL's in-house capacity over the past decade continue to flow alongside campaign-funded capacity.

What the scenario tests is whether a small place-based field station can absorb growth on this scale without becoming a different institution. The basin's long records are protected. The data team grows several-fold. The sensing network that began with SAIL becomes permanent infrastructure RMBL maintains. RMBL365 deepens into a year-round meeting and partnership hub for water managers, Forest Service planners, and tribal natural-resources offices. A cross-mountain partnership network forms with RMBL as the anchor institution.

The central tension is internal. Growth along every dimension at once is harder than growth along one. The loudest risk in this scenario is that RMBL hires too quickly, partners too broadly, or expands its physical footprint past what its culture can carry — and stops feeling like the small basin-anchored nonprofit it has been. The bet the scenario makes is that careful, deliberate expansion across the full campaign horizon leaves RMBL more itself, not less.

## The arc — three phases across the primary horizon

### Phase 1: Early signal and careful absorption (2026–2030)

The campaign's centennial year in 2028 marks the moment when major-gift commitments push the total well past what planning had assumed. Leadership and the board face an early choice: spend the windfall quickly or pace its absorption across the horizon. They choose pacing. The data team grows by a few positions, not by many, with the first new hires focused on archival digitization and synthesis tools for the marmot and phenology records. RMBL365 renovations begin, adding laboratory and staging space without changing the building's footprint. Initial planning for the permanent sensing network gets underway, building on the SAIL inheritance. Operating reserves grow alongside program spending. By 2030, the basin's long records are in a more stable state than they have been in years, and a deliberate hiring plan has been set for the rest of the campaign window. The institution looks recognizably like its 2026 self with somewhat more capacity.

### Phase 2: Integration matures (2031–2035)

The data team reaches a size that lets it partner with guest scientists on synthesis across all four long records at once. AI-assisted retrospective work pulls findings from decades of field notes and demographic records that prior generations could not analyze at this scale. The permanent sensing network across the East River goes live in its first integrated form, coupling atmospheric, snow, hydrologic, and soil measurements at sites where the long ecological records also run. RMBL365 hosts the first year-round meetings of the cross-mountain partnership network, with peer field stations using the basin's records as a comparative reference. Forest Service planners and water managers begin coming to Gothic and to Crested Butte for working sessions tied to specific decisions. By 2035, the campaign has been substantially deployed, growth has been managed without major hiring shocks, and the institution's character is intact.

### Phase 3: Records milestones and steady operation (2036–2040)

In 2038 the marmot demographic study passes its seventy-fifth year. The meadow phenology series approaches sixty-five. RMBL marks both alongside its own twelfth decade. The data team, now several times its 2026 size, has made the basin's long records globally readable — used in climate-attribution work, evolutionary medicine, and mountain-system synthesis at planetary scale. Guest scientists come to Gothic and to RMBL365 because the integrated tools and the place together have become irreplaceable. The cross-mountain network is anchored with RMBL as the convening institution. Operating reserves are at a level that buffers the institution across the next several decades. The basin-anchored nonprofit identity has held: leadership has resisted the pull toward becoming a directive research institution, and the guest-scientist model remains the core of how basin research happens. By 2040, RMBL is recognizably the same institution it was in 2026, with three to four times the operational capacity.

## Lines of inquiry

The phenology work matures further, with the half-century of meadow records now read against integrated atmospheric, snow, and soil measurements at the same sites. Phenology mismatch questions can be asked mechanistically rather than descriptively, because the relevant variables are co-located. The marmot study moves into its eighth decade with molecular tools — methylation clocks, genomic sampling — added to the demographic backbone, letting individual life histories be read at scales prior generations could not reach. The plant-pollinator interaction network is now long enough, and dense enough, that decadal rewiring can be analyzed against the climate record that drove it. The integrated mountain-system science that SAIL began matures into a permanent observational platform, with the East River as one of the few mountain catchments in the world where atmosphere-to-bedrock measurement is sustained across decades. AI-assisted synthesis becomes a generation-defining capability for the basin — not because RMBL hosts the AI research, but because RMBL's records are the substrate on which that synthesis is most productively done. What does not happen: RMBL does not become a center for AI methods research, does not pursue its own independent research agenda, does not expand into systems outside the basin's distinctive contribution.

## Moments of choice within the primary horizon

1. **2028 — How quickly to deploy the campaign as commitments come in well above plan** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the board
   - Alternatives: Spend the windfall on a faster growth schedule that lets the institution show visible progress within the campaign's announcement window, or pace the deployment across the full horizon so that hiring, partnerships, and physical expansion are absorbed at a rate the institution's culture can carry.
   - Scenario assumption: Pacing is chosen. The institution accepts the cost of slower visible progress in exchange for protecting what makes RMBL distinctive.

2. **2029 — Whether to grow the data team primarily through senior hires or through a mix of senior leadership and earlier-career builders** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the data team
   - Alternatives: Hire several senior data scientists quickly to anchor synthesis partnerships, or build the team more slowly around a small senior core with earlier-career staff trained in the basin's specific data tradition.
   - Scenario assumption: The mixed approach is chosen, with hiring spread across several years and explicit attention to building team culture around the basin's records rather than around generic data-science practice.

3. **2030 — Whether to host the cross-mountain partnership network as a formal RMBL program or as a looser convening relationship** *Shared inflection: `cross-mountain-network-form`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and partner institutions
   - Alternatives: Take on the network as a formal RMBL program with staff, governance, and budget responsibility, or convene it as a partnership where RMBL hosts but other institutions share operating responsibility.
   - Scenario assumption: The looser convening model is chosen, keeping RMBL's staff footprint small and avoiding mission drift into being a multi-institution program office.

4. **2032 — How to use RMBL365 as its capacity matures** *Shared inflection: `rmbl365-renovation-scope`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the board
   - Alternatives: Use the building primarily as additional housing and workshop space for the summer research community, or develop it as a year-round community-facing hub for partnerships with water managers, Forest Service planners, and tribal natural-resources offices.
   - Scenario assumption: The year-round, community-facing direction is chosen, in line with RMBL's institutional priority around community service.

5. **2034 — How widely to share AI-assisted synthesis tools built on the basin's records** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, the data team, and guest scientists
   - Alternatives: Keep the tools internal to RMBL and its partners, share them on request with collaborators, or release them broadly as infrastructure other field stations and research groups can use on their own long records.
   - Scenario assumption: Broad release is chosen, with RMBL recognized as the institution that built the tools and the basin's records as the reference dataset on which they were developed.

6. **2037 — How much of the over-performing campaign to lock into long-term savings versus operating expansion** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the board
   - Alternatives: Direct a larger share into reserves that protect the institution's independence across future funding shocks, or direct more into ongoing operations and program staff while the campaign's momentum is fresh.
   - Scenario assumption: A substantial share is locked into reserves, accepting somewhat slower program growth in exchange for institutional resilience across the decades the long records require.

## Audience lens — research

For scientists working on the basin's long records, this scenario means the records are protected, the data systems linking them are richer than they have ever been, and the AI-assisted synthesis tools available at RMBL let questions be asked at scales prior generations could not reach. For scientists working on integrated mountain-system science, the East River becomes one of the few places in the world where atmosphere-to-bedrock measurement runs continuously across decades, with ecological records co-located at the same sites. Guest scientists arriving in Gothic in 2035 find a research community larger and more integrated than the one that preceded it, but still organized around the guest-scientist model: RMBL provides the infrastructure, the tools, the data team, and the place, and the scientific direction stays with the visiting community. The basin remains a place where independent inquiry can continue without restrictions on what questions get asked.

The staff grows from the technical core of 2026 to several times that size, but the additions are concentrated in catalytic capacity — data scientists, geospatial staff, instrumentation technicians, partnership coordinators — rather than in independent research staff. The guest-scientist model holds. RMBL365 deepens into a year-round venue that anchors community partnerships. The basin's long records are in a more stable state than at any time in their history. Operating savings buffer the institution against future funding shocks. The board has spent more attention on institutional culture and pacing decisions than a smaller campaign would have required, and the result is an organization that feels recognizably continuous with what it was in 2026. The risk that RMBL stops feeling like RMBL has been actively managed across the horizon.

## Overlay robustness

**Central case.** Under gradual federal contraction, gradual AI evolution, and middle-range climate change, the scenario plays out as described. Federal grants that built RMBL's in-house capacity over the past decade continue to flow at a reduced but steady rate. AI tools mature in ways that make synthesis of the basin's records progressively more productive. Climate change continues to push the basin's systems past thresholds the long records can document. The campaign's over-performance carries the institution through the federal contraction without forcing painful program cuts, and the savings built into the campaign buffer the next several decades.

**Stress case — AI capability winter.** If AI tools stop maturing — through a capability winter, regulatory restrictions, or compute constraints — the synthesis bet at the heart of this scenario weakens. The data team is still valuable for archival work, data systems, and partnerships with guest scientists, but the generation-defining synthesis capability does not materialize at the scale the scenario assumes. The basin's long records are still protected, RMBL365 still deepens, the cross-mountain network still forms, but RMBL's distinctive contribution to global synthesis is smaller than the upside case projects. The institution is still better off than under a smaller campaign.

**Stress case — Deeper federal contraction.** If federal science funding contracts further than the central case assumes, the outside grants that have built RMBL's in-house capacity over the past decade slow substantially. The campaign's over-performance becomes more strategically necessary, not less, and the savings built into the campaign carry more weight. Pacing decisions made early in the campaign window prove decisive: the institution that paced its growth has reserves to draw on, while the institution that spent the windfall quickly faces hard choices. The scenario's assumption that pacing is chosen is what makes it robust against this stress.

**Stress case — Growth absorption failure.** If RMBL hires too quickly, partners too broadly, or expands its physical footprint past what its culture can carry, the institution starts to feel like a different organization. Senior staff turnover rises. The guest-scientist community begins to feel like outsiders at their own field station. The board faces a difficult mid-campaign correction. This is the loudest internal risk in the scenario and the one the moment-of-choice structure is designed to manage. Whether it materializes depends on board attention and leadership discipline across the full horizon, not on any single decision.

## Plausibility caveats

This scenario is explicitly an upside-tail outcome. It depends on a stack of favorable conditions holding together across the campaign horizon: the campaign substantially exceeds the realistic ceiling through a combination of major-gift, foundation, and individual giving response to the centennial framing; AI and data tools continue to mature without a capability winter; public science funding stays at least flat in real terms; RMBL successfully recruits and retains the data team and partnership staff the magnitude requires; and growth is managed in a way that preserves the institution's basin-anchored character. The loss of any single one of these conditions would substantially reduce the scenario, though it would not collapse it — RMBL would still be better off than under a smaller campaign.

The characteristic failure mode is internal rather than external. Growth along every dimension at once is harder to manage than growth along one, and the risk that RMBL stops feeling like RMBL — that the guest-scientist community begins to feel like outsiders, that senior staff turn over, that the institution drifts toward being a directive research organization — is the loudest risk in the scenario. The moment-of-choice structure tries to manage it, but no scenario structure can substitute for board attention and leadership discipline across the full horizon.

The structural blind spot is the assumption that careful pacing and cultural attention can absorb three times the realistic campaign magnitude without changing what the institution is. That assumption may simply be wrong. There may be a magnitude at which growth itself transforms an institution regardless of how carefully it is managed, and this scenario may be at or past that magnitude. The scenario takes the bet that careful expansion preserves identity; a serious reader can reasonably doubt the bet.
