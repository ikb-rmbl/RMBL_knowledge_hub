# Centennial Hollowed

*A Future Scenario in the `centennial-2027-downside` set. Floor magnitude, continuity-leaning. v1.0 under [Future Scenarios Framework v0.16](../../Future_scenarios_framework.md).*

---

## Synopsis

This is a downside scenario. It assumes the Centennial Campaign closes well below the realistic floor — a downside-tail outcome — at the same time that federal science funding contracts further, costs around Gothic rise, and donors who once gave to environmental science shift their attention elsewhere. RMBL does not collapse. It narrows. The campaign protects what is hardest to replace: the marmot demographic record on the slopes above Gothic, now passing its 75-year mark in 2038, and the meadow phenology series approaching its 65th year. The data team shrinks rather than grows. Partnership work consolidates into a single half-time role. RMBL365 keeps reduced winter hours. Several research lines that ran in the 2020s are paused or handed to peer institutions. What the scenario tests is whether RMBL's core identity can be preserved when scope must contract. It asks donors and the institution to choose the records and the institutional core over surface breadth.

### Campaign deliverables

- **primary.** Protect the two longest basin records — the marmot demographic study and the meadow phenology series — through observer-succession support, archival systems, and the field logistics that let guest scientists continue both lines without lapse. This is the single largest commitment of the campaign and the one the scenario will not compromise.
- **primary.** Build savings that buffer RMBL against further funding shocks. The campaign prioritizes a real operating reserve over new program launches, on the judgment that institutional survival through a hard decade is itself the strategic objective.
- **secondary.** Sustain a smaller technical core — roughly the geospatial and GIS capacity already in place as of 2026, with succession planning but not growth. The campaign funds continuity of these positions rather than expansion.
- **supporting.** Consolidate community and partnership work into a single half-time coordinator role based at RMBL365, with reduced winter hours at the building. The in-town presence continues but at a smaller scale than its 2025 acquisition implied.
- **continuity_residual.** Hand off or pause research lines that the smaller institution cannot carry. Negotiate transfers of some basin-adjacent records to peer institutions where the records can be continued by partners who still have capacity.

### Frontier support strategies

- **F.cont.1.** The campaign protects the records that take longest to rebuild if lost. Funding goes to protocol stewardship, the archival systems that hold the accumulated data, and the field logistics — housing, access, equipment — that let guest scientists carry the work forward. New instrumentation is deferred. The choice is to keep what exists running, not to extend its reach.
- **F.cont.2.** The marmot study is the single most protected line in the scenario. The campaign supports the trapping infrastructure, the data archive, and the housing and logistics on which the guest scientists who carry the record depend. By 2038 the study passes its 75-year mark without a gap. This is the scenario's central commitment.

### Forgone

This scenario does not launch new research frontiers. The atmosphere-to-bedrock integration work that grew out of SAIL continues only as guest scientists bring their own funding; RMBL does not build new permanent infrastructure to host it. AI-assisted synthesis of the long records is deferred — the Knowledge Commons is maintained but not extended, and no new data-science positions are added. Cross-mountain comparative work pauses. Community-science partnerships shrink to what one half-time coordinator can hold. Renovations to RMBL365 beyond basic maintenance are not pursued. Several research lines that ran in the 2020s — including some pollinator and plant work — are either paused at RMBL or handed to peer institutions that still have capacity. The scenario forgoes breadth in order to protect depth. A reader should see this clearly: the choice is to keep the longest records running and the institution intact, at the cost of much of what the campaign in its fuller form might have built.

### Seeds in the present

The marmot study, started in 1963 and now in its eighth decade, is the clearest seed: a record that cannot be reconstructed if interrupted. The meadow phenology series, started in the mid-1970s, is the other. Both depend on guest scientists who in turn depend on RMBL's housing, archives, and field logistics. The Knowledge Commons itself — a product of the recent pattern where outside grants built RMBL's own data tools — is already in place and can be maintained at lower cost than it would take to build again. RMBL365, purchased in 2025, gives RMBL a year-round in-town presence that even at reduced hours is more than the institution had a decade ago. The 2021–25 era primer shows what fuller activity looked like; this scenario describes what protecting the core of that activity looks like when the campaign falls short and federal funding keeps contracting.

## Setting

This scenario assumes a hard decade. The campaign comes in well below the realistic floor. Federal science funding contracts further rather than stabilizing. Operating costs in Gothic rise as private development presses against the gate and lease arrangements tighten. Donor appetite for environmental science philanthropy thins as wealth concentrates in causes further from basin work.

None of these conditions alone would be decisive. Stacked, they force a choice RMBL has not had to make in its recent history: narrow the institution, or risk losing the records that define it. The scenario chooses to narrow.

The shape of that narrowing is specific. The technical core does not grow; it shrinks slightly through attrition and is held at roughly two-thirds its 2026 size. Partnership and community work consolidates into a single half-time role. RMBL365 runs on reduced winter hours. Some research lines that operated in the 2020s are paused or transferred to peer institutions. The campaign builds savings that buffer the institution against the next shock rather than launching new programs.

What the campaign protects, it protects deliberately. The marmot demographic record continues without a gap and passes its 75-year mark in 2038. The meadow phenology series continues and approaches 65 years by 2040. The Knowledge Commons is maintained. The Gothic site remains functional through the summer season.

What this scenario tests is whether RMBL's identity survives contraction. The bet is that the institution can be smaller and still be itself — that protecting the records and the institutional core matters more than maintaining surface breadth. The scenario does not promise this works. It describes what trying looks like.

## The arc — three phases across the primary horizon

### Phase 1: Recognizing the shortfall (2026–2030)

The campaign launches in 2027 but by 2029 it is clear the totals will fall well short of the realistic floor. Federal grants to basin-associated researchers contract further. Two of the technical staff positions in place at the end of 2026 are not replaced when their occupants leave; the data team holds at a smaller size. RMBL leadership and the board make an explicit decision in 2029 to prioritize the marmot study, the meadow phenology series, and operating reserves over new program launches. RMBL365 begins operating on reduced winter hours starting in the 2029–30 season. Conversations open with peer institutions about transferring stewardship of two basin-adjacent research lines that RMBL can no longer support directly. The Knowledge Commons stays online and is maintained, but no new features are added and no new data-science hire is made. Guest scientists who can bring their own funding continue to work at Gothic; those who cannot, in some cases, do not return.

### Phase 2: Living at the smaller scale (2031–2035)

The narrower configuration becomes routine. Partnership and community work runs through a single half-time coordinator role based at RMBL365. Two basin-adjacent research lines are formally transferred to peer institutions in 2032 and 2033 — the records continue, but at other institutions, with RMBL named as a historical partner. The marmot study and the phenology series continue uninterrupted. The Gothic site operates a shortened summer season in 2033 after a wildfire closes one access route for several weeks; guest scientists adapt. The Knowledge Commons is migrated to a lower-cost hosting arrangement that preserves access but reduces the engineering attention it receives. The operating reserve, built deliberately during the campaign, absorbs a federal funding shock in 2034 that would otherwise have forced an additional staff reduction. The institution feels smaller to those who work there. It also feels intact.

### Phase 3: Holding the core through 2038 (2036–2040)

The marmot demographic study passes its 75-year mark in 2038 without a gap. This is the scenario's central achievement. The guest scientists who carry the record do so on infrastructure RMBL kept funded through the hard decade. The meadow phenology series continues and approaches 65 years by 2040. RMBL365 has settled into a stable smaller pattern — open year-round at reduced hours, used by the half-time community coordinator and by occasional workshops. The technical core has stabilized at roughly two-thirds its 2026 size. A modest succession-planning effort begins in 2039 for both of the long records, looking ahead to who will carry them in the 2040s. The institution enters 2040 smaller than it was in 2026, with several research lines now hosted elsewhere, but with its core identity — a place where the longest basin records can still be carried forward — intact.

## Lines of inquiry

Two lines of inquiry mature through the period because they are the ones the campaign chose to protect. The marmot demographic study continues into its eighth decade with the records and the field infrastructure both intact. Questions about how social structure relates to survival, about the molecular tools now being applied to individual life histories, and about how a long-lived hibernating mammal responds to a changing climate all keep advancing — carried by guest scientists, supported by RMBL's smaller technical core. The meadow phenology series continues to deepen the basin's understanding of how flowering, frost, and pollinator activity shift as snow patterns change.

Much else slows or stops at RMBL. The atmosphere-to-bedrock integration work that grew out of SAIL continues only where guest scientists bring their own funding and instruments; RMBL does not extend its hosting infrastructure for this work. AI-assisted synthesis of the long records, which would have required new data-science capacity, is deferred. Cross-mountain comparative work pauses. The pollinator and plant work that ran broadly in the 2020s narrows to what fits inside the protected phenology series. Some research lines that operated at RMBL move to peer institutions and continue there. The basin science community as a whole still produces results; the share of that work hosted at RMBL is smaller than it was in 2026.

## Moments of choice within the primary horizon

1. **2029 — Whether to launch any new programs during the campaign or to redirect the smaller-than-expected totals toward protecting the long records and building operating reserves.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and board
   - Alternatives: Launch a scaled-down version of one new frontier (likely the AI synthesis line); or hold to protecting existing records and savings only.
   - Scenario assumption: The board chooses to protect existing records and build reserves. No new frontier is launched.

2. **2030 — Whether to backfill technical staff positions as they turn over, or to let the data team contract through attrition.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and senior technical staff
   - Alternatives: Backfill at 2026 levels by drawing on reserves; or hold openings and let the team shrink.
   - Scenario assumption: Leadership holds openings. The data team stabilizes at roughly two-thirds its 2026 size.

3. **2032 — Whether to transfer stewardship of two basin-adjacent research lines to peer institutions that still have capacity to carry them.** *Shared inflection: `long-record-stewardship-transfer`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and peer-institution partners
   - Alternatives: Try to keep the lines at RMBL at minimal support; or formalize the transfer so the records continue elsewhere without gaps.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL formalizes the transfers. The records continue at peer institutions, with RMBL named as a historical partner.

4. **2033 — Whether to keep RMBL365 on its original year-round full-hours model or to move to reduced winter hours and a smaller staffing footprint.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and the RMBL365 operations team
   - Alternatives: Subsidize full hours from reserves; or move to reduced hours and protect the reserves for record continuity.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL365 moves to reduced winter hours. A single half-time community coordinator runs the in-town presence.

5. **2034 — Whether to draw down the operating reserve to absorb a federal funding shock or to make a further staff reduction.** *Shared inflection: `reserves-versus-staffing-tradeoff`.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership and board
   - Alternatives: Draw down reserves and risk thinner buffer for the next shock; or reduce staff and keep reserves intact.
   - Scenario assumption: Leadership draws down reserves. The shock is absorbed without further staff loss. The decision to build reserves during the campaign is what makes this possible.

6. **2038 — How to mark the 75th year of the marmot demographic study, and whether to commit publicly to the succession planning needed to carry it into its ninth decade.** *Distinctive to this scenario.*
   - Actors: RMBL leadership, the guest scientists carrying the marmot study, and the broader research community
   - Alternatives: Note the milestone quietly given the smaller institutional moment; or use the milestone to make a visible succession commitment that lays groundwork for the 2040s.
   - Scenario assumption: RMBL uses the milestone to make a visible succession commitment. The record's continuity into the 2040s is named explicitly.

## Audience lens — research

If you bring research to RMBL during this period, you find a smaller institution than the one you might have visited in 2025. The Gothic site is functional through the summer season. Housing is available. The marmot trapping grids and the long phenology plots are intact and the records behind them are unbroken. The Knowledge Commons is online. What you will not find is much new infrastructure, much new technical staff capacity to support novel sensing or AI analysis, or the in-town capacity for year-round work that RMBL365 in its fuller form would have offered. If your work depends on the long records, this is still the place. If your work needs new platforms RMBL would build for it, you are likely doing that work at another institution, with RMBL as a collaborator rather than a host.

## Audience lens — institution

Working at RMBL during this period feels different from the trajectory the 2025 RMBL365 acquisition implied. The institution is smaller. The technical core is the same people doing the same essential maintenance, with fewer of them and less capacity for new projects. The half-time community coordinator at RMBL365 is the visible face of community work, where a fuller program might have grown. Staff conversations in 2034 about the funding shock and whether to draw down reserves are real and hard. What sustains the institution through the decade is a clarity about what it is protecting: the marmot record passing 75 years in 2038, the phenology series approaching 65 by 2040, the institutional capacity to host guest scientists who depend on those records. RMBL feels durable, in a smaller form, because that clarity holds.

## Audience lens — donor

The scenario your gift is part of is one in which RMBL chose, deliberately, to protect what cannot be reconstructed: the marmot demographic study as it passes its 75th year in 2038, the meadow phenology series as it approaches 65, the institutional savings that let RMBL absorb a funding shock in 2034 without losing more staff. You are part of building an institution that is smaller than it might have been, and still itself — a place where the longest basin records continue without a gap through a hard period. The invitation is to help make the narrower version of RMBL durable, on the judgment that durability of the core matters more than breadth.

## Overlay robustness

**Central case.** Under the central case overlay — gradual federal contraction, gradual AI evolution, RCP6.0 climate — the scenario as described is the scenario. Federal funding to basin-associated researchers contracts further. Climate-driven pressure on the Gothic season is real but manageable in most years. AI tools that might have powered new synthesis at RMBL exist but are not adopted at RMBL because the data-science capacity to integrate them is not built. The institution narrows and the long records are protected. This is the configuration the scenario is written for.

**Stress case — Sharper federal contraction.** If federal contraction is sharper than expected, the funding shock in 2034 is larger and the reserves built during the campaign do not fully cover it. A further staff reduction follows in 2035. The marmot study and phenology series still continue because they are protected by the explicit campaign priority, but the technical core thins below the level at which the Knowledge Commons can be reliably maintained. By 2040 the Commons may be in archival-only mode. The institution survives but with less capacity to support guest scientists who depend on its data systems.

**Stress case — Faster climate acceleration.** If climate change advances faster than RCP6.0, the Gothic season is interrupted in more years by fire, smoke, or access issues. The phenology series begins to record a system shifting beyond its historical range — scientifically valuable, but operationally harder to maintain. One or more of the long-running plots may be compromised. The marmot study remains feasible because the slopes above Gothic are less fire-exposed than the meadow plots, but observer access becomes less reliable. The scenario's core commitments hold; their execution gets harder.

**Stress case — Donor appetite recovers partway.** If donor interest in environmental science recovers partway through the decade, a late-campaign push in 2032–34 brings totals up somewhat — still below the realistic floor, but enough to add a single additional technical position or restore RMBL365 to fuller hours. The institution remains narrower than the central case but has a little more operating room. The transfers of research lines to peer institutions are not reversed; the records have settled in their new homes.

## Plausibility caveats

This scenario rests on several assumptions worth naming. It assumes the campaign falls well below its realistic floor — not that it collapses, but that it underperforms in a sustained way across the period. It assumes federal funding keeps contracting rather than stabilizing or partly recovering. It assumes operating costs around Gothic rise and donor appetite for environmental science thins. None of these conditions is itself implausible; the scenario depends on them stacking, which is a strong joint assumption.

The characteristic way this scenario could go wrong, given its central bet, is this: protecting the records and the institutional core may not be enough to preserve identity if the surrounding research community erodes. The marmot study and the phenology series depend on guest scientists who depend in turn on a wider ecosystem of basin science. If too much of that ecosystem moves to peer institutions, the protected records continue but in a thinner intellectual context, and RMBL's identity as a basin science hub may erode even as the records themselves are intact. The scenario assumes the core is enough to anchor the wider community. It might not be.

The structural blind spot is that the scenario describes a managed contraction. Real contractions are often less orderly. Staff departures may cluster rather than smooth; funding shocks may arrive in years when reserves are already partly drawn down; the 2034 shock the scenario assumes is absorbed cleanly might not be. The scenario also does not deeply model what happens if the guest scientists who currently carry the long records reach retirement during a period when RMBL cannot help recruit successors. Succession planning is named as a 2039 action; whether it works is outside the scenario's resolution.

## Looking further out: undefined–undefined

*Speculative. Lower resolution than the primary horizon.*

Through the 2040s the picture grows hazier. If the scenario's bet works, RMBL enters the decade smaller but stable, with the marmot study and phenology series continuing under successor scientists and the institutional core intact. The records that were protected through the hard 2030s become, in the 2040s, the basis on which RMBL can begin to grow again — modestly, opportunistically, as funding conditions allow. The two basin-adjacent research lines transferred to peer institutions in the early 2030s continue at those institutions; some collaboration with RMBL resumes. RMBL365 may expand its hours again if community demand and resources align.

If the bet does not work — if the wider research community erodes, if succession on the long records fails, if another funding shock arrives before reserves can be rebuilt — the 2040s could see a further narrowing, with RMBL holding only the most essential infrastructure and acting more as a custodian of records than as an active basin science hub. The scenario does not predict which. It describes what the institution did during the hard decade to give itself a chance at the better path.
